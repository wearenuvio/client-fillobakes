# Teardown 7 — Voice & Personality Systems (Out-of-Category D2C)

UX Research Phase 2 of 5 · Fillo Bakes (Japanese eggless moving-bakery van, Bengaluru · playful-warm · weekly drop model)

**Method.** Live pages fetched Aug 2026 via WebFetch (homepage + FAQ + PDP + about, where the route existed). Every quote below is verbatim from the page indicated. Where a line came from a secondary source (an ad or a package, not a live page), it is marked `[secondary]` with attribution. Sites where a route 404'd are noted — the analysis then rests on what did load.

**Why out-of-category.** Bakery sites almost universally default to one of two voices: precious-artisanal ("hand-laminated, 72-hour ferment") or transactional-Shopify ("Add to cart"). Neither is a *system*. The seven brands below have systems — rules for where humour lives, where it is banned, and what the joke is *about*. That system is the transferable asset, not the jokes.

## 1. Liquid Death — liquiddeath.com

**Fetched:** homepage, `/pages/faq`, `/collections/all`

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Deadpan · Committed · Literal-minded.**

The voice is a single sustained bit — "water, but marketed like heavy metal" — executed with total commitment and zero winking. Crucially it is not *mean*; it is absurd.

> "First, congrats. We can't wait to meet the little demon. Second, we definitely recommend drinking Mountain Water while pregnant because water is even more important when you're hydrating for two."
> — liquiddeath.com/pages/faq, "I'M PREGNANT. CAN I DRINK LIQUID DEATH?"

> "All merch orders from our site of $85+ ship for free on the wings of two-headed demon eagles. Orders under $85 have a standard shipping rate of $6 to help pay the salary of our two-headed demon eagles."
> — liquiddeath.com/pages/faq, shipping

> "Sucks. We can help you with that."
> — liquiddeath.com/pages/faq, "I CAN'T LOG INTO MY ACCOUNT"

**Where humour lives: the FAQ.** This is the single most important structural finding of this teardown. Liquid Death's homepage is *headline-loud* but not funny ("Murder Your Thirst"). The genuinely funny writing is buried in the support content — shipping costs, login failures, receipt rejection, gift cards. The joke is placed exactly where the user is bored or annoyed.

Note the two-headed demon eagle line: it delivers **the real number twice** ($85 threshold, $6 rate) and wraps the joke around it. The joke never replaces the fact; it escorts it.

### (b) Copy structure
Product benefit is written as **spec, then joke-as-payload**:

> "Most bottled water on the market comes in 20 oz servings. To deliver more Liquid Death to you and thus more efficiently chainsaw your thirst to bits, some flavors come in 19.2oz cans."

Sequence is: category norm → our deviation → why (in-voice) → the number. Even the sustainability claim runs on hard facts: *"of all the aluminum produced since 1888, 75% of it is still currently in use."*

Product names are the compression layer — the entire brand lives in "Rest In Peach", "Grave Fruit", "Squeezed To Death", "Cherry Obituary". A name does more voice-work per pixel than any paragraph.

**CTA verbs:** `SHOP NOW` · `WATCH VIDEO` · `JOIN THE CLUB` · `SELL LIQUID DEATH` · `Add to Cart` · `Notify me when available`. Note: the *marketing* CTAs are branded ("JOIN THE CLUB", "Earn Skulls"); the *transactional* CTAs are stock Shopify.

### (c) Personality vs usability
Ruthlessly disciplined. The collections page filters are entirely plain: `SORT BY FEATURED`, `ALPHABETICALLY, A-Z`, `PRICE, LOW TO HIGH`, `shop by price`, `shop by size`, `reset apply`. Out-of-stock is `Sold out` / `Notify me when available`. No skulls, no demons.

The return policy is written in flat bureaucratic bullets — *"Items must be returned unworn, unwashed and undamaged"* — with no jokes at all. **Money-back copy is a joke-free zone.** So is anything a customer would screenshot in a complaint.

### (d) Psychology
- **Identity/tribe** — "our ever-growing cult", "Country Club", Skulls as currency. You are not a buyer, you are a member.
- **Rebellion-by-proxy** — the merch is "clothes your mom will definitely hate". Low-cost transgression.
- **Honesty-as-charm** — the plastic-liner FAQ answer volunteers an inconvenient fact ("all cans use an ultra thin microscopic liner") and contextualises it rather than dodging.
- **Contrast/absurdity** — the entire brand is one long incongruity joke: the most boring product on earth (water) in the least boring wrapper.

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Put the warmth in the support copy, not the hero.** Fillo should keep the homepage calm and appetising, and spend its personality budget on the FAQ, the shipping line, and the error states. Rewrite the delivery-fee line the way Liquid Death rewrote theirs: state the number, then add one warm human sentence about the van. *"Delivery inside the ring road is ₹49. Beyond that it's ₹79, because the van has to go the long way round and Kenji drives carefully with laminated things."* The fact stays first, the warmth rides along.

## 2. Graza — graza.co

**Fetched:** homepage, `/pages/faqs`, `/pages/about-us`, `/products/sizzle`

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Plainspoken · Nerdy-warm · Unpretentious.**

Graza sells a category (olive oil) that is *drowning* in pretension, and its entire voice strategy is refusal-of-pretension. It is the only brand here whose humour is fundamentally *gentle*.

> "Just like (most) humans, olives get more chill with age."
> — graza.co/products/sizzle, spec panel

> "Young olives are full of attitude."
> — graza.co/products/sizzle (Drizzle panel)

> "Made for eating, never heating."
> — graza.co homepage, Drizzle

> "Color is truly not an indicator of an olive oil's quality... but like all the best things in life, color is fleeting."
> — graza.co/pages/faqs

**Where humour lives: the spec labels and the FAQ.** Graza's jokes are *inside* the technical facts, not next to them. "TAKES IT EASY" sits directly above "13LBS OF OLIVES = 1 LITER OF OIL". Personality and data occupy the same module.

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is written as **use-case first, spec second.** Not "cold-pressed single-varietal Picual" but *"Your new high heat kitchen hero"* and *"Use it every day, in every way."* The three-product line is named by *behaviour* — Drizzle / Sizzle / Frizzle — so the product name teaches the use case. A customer who learns three rhyming words has learned the whole range.

The FAQ is where the nerd comes out and it earns trust: *"Approximately 97% of Graza olive oil is made from Picual olives that are picked, pressed, and bottled in Jaen, Spain."* Precision (97%, not "most") reads as honesty.

**CTA verbs:** `SHOP NOW` · `Shop All ➛` · `Learn More About Us` · `Manage Subscription` · `Subscribe`. Almost aggressively normal.

### (c) Personality vs usability
Shipping, returns and subscription copy are functional with one warm sentence bolted on:

> "Unfortunately, we are not able to accept returns whereas Graza is a perishable food product. However, if you have any issues whatsoever with your order, we will always work to make it right!"

The structure is **hard boundary + human promise**. The "no" is unambiguous; the recovery is warm. This is the single best-engineered sentence in this teardown for a perishable-goods brand — and Fillo sells perishable goods.

Also: *"We tested hundreds of funnels, and we can confidently recommend this Oxo one right here."* Recommending a competitor's product, unprompted, is an enormous trust move.

### (d) Psychology
- **Honesty-as-charm** (dominant) — "we hope to bring Graza to the world eventually" instead of a vague international-shipping dodge.
- **Reciprocity** — the funnel recommendation, the freezing-your-oil answer (which reassures rather than upsells).
- **Competence signalling without gatekeeping** — deep sourcing detail delivered in casual register ("easy-peasy refilling").
- **No scarcity at all.** Graza is the only brand here that runs zero urgency. Its persuasion is entirely trust-based.

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Steal the "hard boundary + human promise" return sentence, verbatim in structure.** Fillo's hardest usability moment is the perishability + weekly-drop combination: no returns, fixed pickup window, sells out. Write it Graza's way:

> *"We can't take returns — everything we bake is made for the day it's made. But if anything's wrong with your box, tell us and we'll fix it. Always."*

Second move: **name products by behaviour, not by grandeur.** Graza's Drizzle/Sizzle/Frizzle teaches the range in three syllables each. A Fillo range named for *moment* rather than technique ("the 4pm one", "the lunchbox one") would do more work than "Hokkaido milk bread with 12-hour tangzhong".

## 3. Fishwife — eatfishwife.com

**Fetched:** homepage, `/pages/our-story`, `/collections/all`, `/products/the-starter-pack`

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Chatty · Enthusiastic · Texting-a-friend.**

Fishwife's voice is the closest of the seven to a *person* rather than a *bit*. It is high-energy, exclamation-heavy, and uses abbreviations that a brand style guide would normally forbid.

> "LMK When it's Back!"
> — eatfishwife.com/products/the-starter-pack, sold-out CTA

> "Time to add some tins!"
> — eatfishwife.com homepage

> "Let's go fishing!"
> — eatfishwife.com homepage

> "Join 1 Million+ Happy Fishwives"
> — eatfishwife.com homepage

**Where humour lives: the button labels.** This is the most distinctive finding here. Fishwife barely tells jokes in prose — the personality is almost entirely in *microcopy*: `LMK When it's Back!`, `Tell me when it's back`, `Just Dropped!`, `Pick your protein!`, `Quick Add`, `See Options`, `Get Started`. The site sounds like a person because the *buttons* sound like a person.

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is written as **story-of-use.** The Starter Pack description is a mini-narrative of the shopper's journey:

> "Looking for the perfect assortment for diving into the world of tinned fish? The Fishwife Starter Pack is the place to start. It's a little bit of everything, from smoky to bright and citrusy, so you or your lucky recipient can discover new favorites and come back for more."

Then a dedicated **How to Eat** block: *"Enjoy tossed into a savory pasta, over warm rice with creamy avocado, tossed into a crisp salad, or layered into a crusty bread sandwich with fresh greens."* For an unfamiliar product, telling people *what to do with it* is the benefit. Fishwife treats occasion-instruction as a first-class content type.

Positioning is a flat claim, no hedging: *"The World's Tastiest Tinned Fish."*

**CTA verbs:** `Shop Fish` · `Explore More` · `Add` · `Quick Add` · `See Options` · `Get Started` · `Build Your Own Bundle` · `LMK When it's Back!`

### (c) Personality vs usability
Fishwife is the *least* disciplined of the seven — it lets voice into the transactional layer (`LMK When it's Back!` is a live button). It gets away with this because the register is warm rather than jokey; nobody is confused about what the button does. The failure mode to avoid: a warm button label that obscures the action. `LMK When it's Back!` still contains the entire action ("back" = restock).

The genuinely boring parts stay boring — `Free Shipping On Orders $75+`, `This site is protected by hCaptcha`.

### (d) Psychology
- **Belonging** — "Join 1 Million+ Happy Fishwives" turns customers into a named collective noun. The brand name *is* the community name.
- **Scarcity + restock ritual** — `Sold Out` / `Back in Stock!` / `Just Dropped!` / `Sardine Dream Hair Clips are Back!` runs a permanent drop cadence, with the restock announced as *news*.
- **Reducing novice anxiety** — "New to Fishwife? Try The Starter Pack!" names the hesitant user and hands them a single door.
- **Provenance/credibility** — "Meet Our Canneries", "Responsibly sourced, exquisitely packed".

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Build the sold-out state as a designed, warm, three-part moment.** Fishwife has `Sold Out` (status) + `Tell me when it's back` (promise) + `Back in Stock!` / `Just Dropped!` (payoff). A weekly-drop bakery will show "sold out" more often than "buy" — it is Fillo's *most-viewed* state and must be the most carefully written string on the site, not an afterthought.

Second move: **steal the "How to Eat" block.** Japanese bakery items are unfamiliar to most Bengaluru shoppers. A short *"how to eat this"* line — warm it 8 minutes, tear don't slice, eat it the same day — converts unfamiliarity into confidence and is more persuasive than any adjective.

## 4. OATLY — oatly.com

**Fetched:** homepage (en-us), `/en-us/things-we-do`, `/en-us/things-we-do/brainwashing`, `/en-us/things-we-do/brainwashing/ifaqs`

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Rambling · Self-aware · Sincerely absurd.**

Oatly's voice is a human being thinking out loud on the page, including the false starts. It sounds *unedited* — which is, of course, the most edited effect on this list.

> "Hi there, and welcome to the world's hugest and only compendium of Infrequently Asked Questions about oatmilk."
> — oatly.com/en-us/things-we-do/brainwashing/ifaqs

> "This really depends on whether we're talking out-of-carton oatmilk or in-carton oatmilk, the density differentials... so let's parking lot this one for now, cool?"
> — ifaqs, "Does oatmilk float?"

> "We can't speak directly for oats, but since they don't have mouths or brains and can't feel pain, we will anyway: 'No'."
> — ifaqs, "Are any oats harmed in the making of oatmilk?"

> "You've probably seen these adverts plastered all over your city or television and thought, 'I can't tell if I hate these or love these, but I'm intrigued by this oat stuff they speak of.' Well, that's sort of what we were going for."
> — oatly.com/en-us/things-we-do/brainwashing

That last line is the whole system in miniature: the brand narrates its own marketing strategy to you, in the marketing.

`[secondary]` Their most-cited ad line — *"ANOTHER AD FOR OUR OAT DRINK PROVIDING NO REASON AT ALL WHY YOU SHOULD TRY IT"* — and the tone triad *"Humorous / Conversational / Intellectual"* from their brand book are quoted in copystyleguide.com's Oatly analysis, not from a live oatly.com page.

**Where humour lives: a dedicated, quarantined section.** Oatly's structural genius is that the deep weirdness has its own address — `/things-we-do/brainwashing/` — labelled honestly as *brainwashing*. Product pages stay clean and factual (*"Our oat base is just oats and water. But it's what we do with those oats and that water that makes Oatly so special."*). Navigation is boring: Products, Health, Sustainability, FAQ, Careers.

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is written as **objection-demolition**. Look at what the 45 IFAQs are actually doing: every absurd question is a proxy for one real anxiety — *"is switching to plant milk going to be a hassle, and will it make me a certain kind of person?"*

> "Learning, ugh. Oatmilk doesn't require any intellectual heavy lifting, so you can remain as oblivious to it as you like."

> "Yes, in fact we encourage maintaining the same bowl routine you use now to minimize the risk of even infinitesimal changes to your lifestyle."

> "Many people yes! Some people no! And a bunch of people might not know what to think about you, which is totally fine because who cares."

The joke format lets them repeat "this requires nothing of you" forty-five times without it reading as forty-five repetitions. Comedy as **frequency laundering** — the same message, endlessly restated, never boring.

And the real benefit is smuggled in through the punchline: *"oatmilk has some really great health aspects like beta-glucans and some really great environmental aspects like on average having a lower climate impact than cow milk."* Note the hedge — "on average" — appears in almost every climate claim. The jokiness never contaminates the accuracy.

**CTA verbs:** `Show more` · plus flat nav. Oatly's CTAs are the plainest here; the brand is not a DTC store, so utility copy carries no load and gets no jokes.

### (c) Personality vs usability
Total separation. Product data, sustainability figures, FAQ, careers, legal, cookie consent — all neutral. The absurdism is confined to a URL path you have to choose to enter. **The weird stuff is opt-in.**

### (d) Psychology
- **Reducing switching cost** — the core persuasion is "this changes nothing about your life", delivered as comedy.
- **Honesty-as-charm** — calling their own campaign section "brainwashing"; conceding *"Some people no!"*
- **Identity-anxiety defusal** — "who cares" as an explicit release valve for the reader worried about being seen as a certain type.
- **Distinctiveness/memory** — the voice is unmistakable in a category with no sensory differentiation.

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Build a Fillo "IFAQ" — but tune it to warmth, not absurdity.** The mechanic to steal is not the surrealism; it is *using a light format to answer forty real anxieties without sounding defensive*. Fillo's anxieties are specific and numerous: is eggless bread dry? is it too sweet? will it survive an auto ride? is it worth ₹X for a small loaf? do I have to be there at exactly 5pm? Written straight, that page reads defensive. Written with a light touch, it reads generous.

> *"Does eggless mean it'll be dry?"* → *"That's the usual worry, and it's fair. We use a Japanese milk-bread method (tangzhong) that holds water in the dough, so it stays soft for about three days. Squeeze the bag before you believe us."*

Second move: **the quarantine.** Give the playfulness one home — a `/notes` or `/from the van` section — so the shop pages can stay calm and appetising. Personality does not have to be evenly distributed to be strong. It has to be *findable*.

## 5. Who Gives A Crap — whogivesacrap.org (→ au.)

**Fetched:** au.whogivesacrap.org homepage, `/pages/about-us`

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Cheeky · Purposeful · Self-deprecating.**

The brand runs a permanent double meaning (toilet humour ↔ actual giving-a-crap) and the whole voice system rests on that single pun being load-bearing rather than decorative.

> "We're here to uncrap the world"
> — au.whogivesacrap.org/pages/about-us

> "It all started with 50 hours on a toilet and a dream"
> — au.whogivesacrap.org/pages/about-us

> "Stay up to date on the latest news, promos and fart jokes"
> — au.whogivesacrap.org, newsletter signup

> "Who knew changing the world could be as easy as changing your toilet paper?"
> — au.whogivesacrap.org homepage

> "Three cheers for giving back, shaking bums and making stuff that makes a difference"
> — au.whogivesacrap.org/pages/about-us

**Where humour lives: the newsletter signup and the founder story.** "the latest news, promos and fart jokes" is the highest-value string on the site — it converts a dreaded ask (give us your email) into a joke that also *sets expectations honestly* about what the emails will contain. The founder story ("50 hours on a toilet") makes the origin *funny and true* simultaneously.

Note also `"Ok, toot toot"` — a self-deprecating sign-off after a quality claim. The brand undercuts its own boast rather than letting it stand. That's a British/Australian move that reads extremely well in Indian English too.

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is **product spec + moral consequence, in one line**:

> "Toilet paper that builds toilets"

Six words containing category, product, and impact. The pattern repeats: *"Doing good has never felt better"*, *"Small choices make a big difference"*, *"The stuff you buy should work well, look good and not destroy the planet."*

The impact numbers are stated as hard facts and never joked about: *"50% of profits are donated"*, *"over $20 million raised to date!"*, *"Every day, 1 million trees are cut down to make traditional toilet paper."* **The mission is the one thing that gets zero irony.**

**CTA verbs:** `Shop now` · `Learn more` · `Shop all` · `Shop bundles` · `Save 20% with Bundles`. Entirely plain.

### (c) Personality vs usability
Product names are aggressively literal — "100% Bamboo Toilet Paper", "100% Recycled Garbage Bags". A brand this jokey names its products with total plainness, because the product name is where a shopper is deciding, not enjoying. Certification marks (Certified B Corp™, FSC®) are presented straight.

### (d) Psychology
- **Warm-glow altruism** — "Doing good has never felt better" makes the *feeling* the product benefit.
- **Effortless virtue** — "as easy as changing your toilet paper" removes the sacrifice framing from ethical purchase.
- **Honesty-as-charm** — "toot toot" pre-empts the reader's eye-roll at self-praise by doing the eye-roll first.
- **Social proof via scale** — "$20 million raised to date".

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Write the newsletter/WhatsApp opt-in as the funniest string on the site, and make the joke also be the expectation-setting.** For a weekly-drop bakery the drop-alert list *is* the business. Do not write "Subscribe for updates."

> *"Get the Sunday message: what we're baking, where the van will be, and one photo of something that didn't work out."*

That is WGAC's move exactly — a joke that is simultaneously an honest content promise. Second move: **one plain six-word line that fuses product and purpose.** WGAC has "Toilet paper that builds toilets." Fillo's equivalent — a line fusing *Japanese method*, *eggless*, and *mobile* — is worth a week of work on its own.

## 6. Surreal — eatsurreal.co.uk

**Fetched:** homepage. (`/pages/about-us`, `/pages/our-story` 404'd; famous billboard campaign referenced via press below.)

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Dry · Footnoted · Confidently silly.**

Surreal's signature device is the **asterisk** — a straight claim followed by a small-print correction that is the actual joke. Their famous 2023 billboards ran *"We're Dwayne Johnson's favourite cereal"* in huge type with a byline noting Dwayne is a bus driver from London (`[secondary]` — Creative Bloq, *"Cereal brand's hilarious celebrity billboards go viral"*). That structure — big honest-sounding claim, small honest correction — is the whole voice.

On-site, the same dryness runs through:

> "Zero Sugar" / "Yep zero, zilch, nada"
> — eatsurreal.co.uk homepage, benefit tile

> "No tigers were hurt in the making of this Surreal"
> — eatsurreal.co.uk, Frosted product description

> "For new flavours, tasty bargains, and sexy bowl cuts. Maybe."
> — eatsurreal.co.uk footer, newsletter signup

> "We'll never share your data. Promise."
> — eatsurreal.co.uk footer

**Where humour lives: the footnote layer and the newsletter signup.** Same finding as WGAC — the email capture is the funniest string. And note the trailing *"Maybe."* — a one-word undercut appended to a promise, which does the same anti-boast work as WGAC's "toot toot."

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is **hard spec, presented as a scannable tile grid, with one dry aside per tile.** "High Protein / 12-14g of plant protein per bowl". "Low Carb / Only 6-8g of net carbs per bowl". "Zero Sugar / Yep zero, zilch, nada." The numbers are precise and the humour is the *garnish on the number*, exactly like Graza.

The positioning line is genuinely excellent and completely straight:

> "The cereal you loved growing up, made nutritionally relevant to the adult you've become."

> "Surreal is the perfect breakfast (or midnight snack) for your inner child."

Note the parenthetical — the aside as a place to be human without derailing the sentence. Surreal uses parentheses and asterisks the way other brands use exclamation marks.

**CTA verbs:** `SHOP NOW` · `Shop All Flavours` · `Build A Box` · `Subscribe & Save` · `CHECKOUT` · `Try risk free with our 30 day money-back guarantee`.

### (c) Personality vs usability
`CHECKOUT` is all-caps and stone plain. The guarantee is stated without a joke anywhere near it: *"Try risk free with our 30 day money-back guarantee"*. Subscription terms are listed as flat benefit bullets — *"20% Off Every Order" / "Early Access" / "Pause, Skip or Cancel"* — with no comedy at all. **Money and commitment are joke-free.** Third brand in a row with this rule.

`"We'll never share your data. Promise."` is the one exception, and it isn't really a joke — it's a reassurance delivered in a human register. Different thing.

### (d) Psychology
- **Nostalgia + permission** — "your inner child", "childhood taste with a grown-up reboot". The purchase is licensed, not justified.
- **Reactance-avoidance via humour** — a health product that doesn't lecture. The jokes signal "we are not going to be smug about this."
- **Risk reversal** — the 30-day guarantee sits right next to the CTA.
- **Anti-corporate underdog** — the celebrity campaign's entire premise is "we can't afford real celebrities", stated proudly.

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Adopt the asterisk/parenthesis as Fillo's humour container.** This is the single most adaptable device in the whole teardown for a *warm* brand, because it never hijacks the main sentence — the headline stays appetising and clear, and the small text carries the personality. It also scales down beautifully to mobile, where a jokey headline eats the whole screen but a footnote costs one line.

> *"Baked fresh this morning.\*"*
> *\*4:15am, which we're still coming to terms with.*

> *"Sold out in 40 minutes (sorry) (thank you)"*

Second move: **the one-word undercut.** "Maybe." / "Promise." / "Sorry." A single trailing word after a claim makes a brand sound like a person rather than a marketing department, and costs nothing.

## 7. Paper Boat — paperboatdrinks.com (→ hectorbeverages.com)

**Fetched:** hectorbeverages.com. (Sleepy Owl fetched as a comparison point — see note.)

### (a) Voice system
**Three adjectives: Nostalgic · Lowercase-gentle · Lyrical.**

Paper Boat is the odd one out and the most directly relevant to Fillo: it is the only brand here whose personality is **warmth without jokes.** It does not do comedy at all. It does memory.

> "drinks and memories"
> — hectorbeverages.com, positioning line

> "When we were little / We knew so very little / Laughed until we cried"
> — hectorbeverages.com

> "Let's go back in time, to a time, / Where there was no watch to watch the time."
> — hectorbeverages.com

> "To days so carefree, that all we needed was to simply be."
> — hectorbeverages.com

**Where personality lives: the navigation labels.** This is Paper Boat's most stealable move. The nav does not read `Products / About / Careers`. It reads:

> `drinks` · `food` · `memories` · `unfolding paperboat` · `sail with us` · `life @ paper boat`

`memories` is a top-level nav item. `sail with us` is the careers page. `unfolding paperboat` is the about page. The brand metaphor (a paper boat) generates the entire information architecture. And everything is lowercase — a typographic decision doing tone-of-voice work with zero words.

Category labels are also plain-poetic rather than corporate: *"the things we did"*, *"the games we played"*, *"once upon a time"*, *"why paper boat"*.

**Indian-brand comparison:** Sleepy Owl (sleepyowl.co) sits at the opposite end — mostly conventional D2C copy ("India's Most Trusted Coffee Brand", "Add to cart", "Shop now") with occasional bursts of personality: *"Brew overnight & Smooooth AF."* and *"Warning: Strong coffee"*. The contrast is instructive: Sleepy Owl's personality is **sporadic and tonally inconsistent** (a trust claim next to "AF"), while Paper Boat's is **structural** — it lives in the IA and the typography, so it holds even where the words are ordinary. Structural personality survives; sprinkled personality doesn't.

### (b) Copy structure
Benefit is written as **story, almost never spec.** The product does not get described; the *memory the product belongs to* gets described. This is a real risk for a functional category — you'll notice the actual product information ("Manufacturing Details") is relegated and dry — but for an emotional purchase it is extremely effective, and it is the only voice on this list that works in India without translation friction.

**CTA verbs:** `give us a follow` · `sail with us`. Lowercase, invitational, no imperatives shouted.

### (c) Personality vs usability
The weak point of this teardown. Paper Boat's poetry-first approach leaves shopping information thin — a lesson in the opposite direction from Liquid Death. Warmth is not a substitute for a clear price, a clear stock state and a clear delivery promise. Fillo should take Paper Boat's *tone* and Graza's *information discipline*.

### (d) Psychology
- **Nostalgia/identity** — the strongest emotional lever available in the Indian market, and Paper Boat owns it.
- **Belonging through shared memory** — "the games we played" assumes a common childhood; the reader is inside the "we" before they buy anything.
- **Sensory-emotional transfer** — the drink is a delivery vehicle for a feeling.
- **No scarcity, no rebellion, no honesty-flex.** Pure warmth. Proof that a strong voice needs none of the edgy levers.

### (e) Transferable move for Fillo
**Let the brand metaphor build the navigation.** Fillo's central image is a *moving van on a weekly route*. That is a gift — it generates IA for free, and it's warm without being cutesy:

- `this week's bake` (shop)
- `where we'll be` (route/locations)
- `from the van` (notes/blog)
- `ride with us` (careers)
- `the long proof` (about — the story of the method)

Second move: **lowercase, or at least sentence case, everywhere.** Six of the seven brands here shout in caps at some point. A Japanese-influenced bakery should not. Quietness is a legitimate personality choice and it is *differentiating* in a category of `SHOP NOW`.

## Voice Spectrum

Two axes. Horizontal: **how loud the personality is.** Vertical: **what the humour is made of** — irony/distance at the top, sincerity/warmth at the bottom.

```
                        IRONY · DISTANCE · THE BIT
                                  │
   Liquid Death ●                 │
                                  │        ● Oatly
                                  │
              Surreal ●           │
                                  │
   ───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────
   QUIET                          │                          LOUD
   ───────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────
                                  │
                     Graza ●      │   ● Who Gives A Crap
                                  │
              ★ FILLO             │        ● Fishwife
                                  │
   Paper Boat ●                   │
                                  │
                     SINCERITY · WARMTH · THE PERSON
```

**Reading the map.**

| Brand | Position | One-line signature |
|---|---|---|
| Liquid Death | Top-left→centre: maximum irony, medium volume | A committed bit, quarantined from checkout |
| Oatly | Top-right: maximum irony, maximum volume | Rambling absurdism in a dedicated URL |
| Surreal | Upper-centre: dry irony, medium volume | The asterisk — big claim, small correction |
| Who Gives A Crap | Lower-right: warm, loud | One pun carrying product + purpose |
| Fishwife | Lower-right: warm, loud | Personality lives in the buttons |
| Graza | Lower-centre-left: warm, quiet | Jokes inside the specs; radical honesty |
| Paper Boat | Bottom-left: warm, quietest | Nostalgia; metaphor builds the IA |

### Where Fillo should sit

**Lower-left quadrant — sincere and quiet — but a step to the right of Paper Boat, and a step up toward Graza.**

Concretely, Fillo's coordinates are: **Paper Boat's register, Graza's structure, Fishwife's microcopy, Surreal's footnote device.**

Why not further right (louder):
- The product is delicate and the aesthetic is Japanese. Loud copy fights the photography and the craft claim. A brand that laminates dough for 12 hours should not shout.
- Bengaluru's premium-bakery audience is over-exposed to exclamation-heavy D2C. Quietness reads as confidence and as *expensive*.
- A one-van operation cannot sustain a high-maintenance comedy voice across weekly drops. Warmth is cheap to maintain; jokes are not. This is a real operational constraint on voice choice.

Why not further left (as quiet as Paper Boat):
- Paper Boat's poetry-first approach starves the shopping layer. Fillo must communicate stock state, pickup window, route, and price with total clarity every single week.
- "Playful" is in the brand ambition. Fillo needs a device for play — the footnote/parenthesis (Surreal) and the warm button (Fishwife) supply it without raising the volume.

Why not up (ironic):
- Irony creates distance. A bakery's core promise is *care*, and irony is the one register that cannot express care. Every edgy brand here is selling a commodity where distance is an asset (water, cereal, toilet paper). Fillo is selling something someone made this morning.

**The formula:** *warm, specific, slightly self-deprecating, never shouting; humour lives in footnotes, buttons, sold-out states and the newsletter opt-in; the shop layer stays plain.*

## 10 Microcopy Patterns Worth Adapting

Each with the source pattern, the mechanic, and a Fillo-voiced draft. These are drafts to test, not final strings.

### 1. The sold-out state as a three-part ritual
**Source:** Fishwife — `Sold Out` → `Tell me when it's back` / `LMK When it's Back!` → `Back in Stock!` / `Just Dropped!`
**Mechanic:** Status, promise, and payoff are three separately written strings. The disappointment is immediately converted into an action, and the return is announced as good news.
**Why it matters most for Fillo:** on a weekly-drop model, sold-out is the *default* state for five days out of seven. It is the most-read copy on the site.
**Fillo draft:**
- Status: `Gone for this week`
- Promise: `Tell me when the van's back out` (button) · helper: *"One message, Sunday morning. Nothing else."*
- Payoff: `Fresh out of the oven` / `This week's bake is up`

### 2. The joke that rides on the number, never replaces it
**Source:** Liquid Death — *"$85+ ship for free on the wings of two-headed demon eagles. Orders under $85 have a standard shipping rate of $6."*
**Mechanic:** Fact → warmth → fact repeated. The reader can extract the number without reading the joke.
**Fillo draft:** *"Free delivery over ₹800. Under that it's ₹60, which is roughly what it costs to get a box across Bengaluru without it becoming a different shape."*

### 3. Hard boundary, human promise
**Source:** Graza — *"we are not able to accept returns whereas Graza is a perishable food product. However, if you have any issues whatsoever with your order, we will always work to make it right!"*
**Mechanic:** The "no" is unambiguous and reason-backed; the recovery sentence is warm and unconditional. Never soften the no — soften the aftermath.
**Fillo draft:** *"We can't take returns. Everything is baked for the day it's made. But if your box arrives wrong, squashed, or just not right — message us and we'll sort it. Every time."*

### 4. The newsletter opt-in as the funniest, most honest string on the site
**Source:** Who Gives A Crap — *"Stay up to date on the latest news, promos and fart jokes."* Surreal — *"For new flavours, tasty bargains, and sexy bowl cuts. Maybe."*
**Mechanic:** The joke *is* the expectation-setting. Telling people exactly what they'll get, in a funny way, converts better than "Subscribe for updates" and reduces unsubscribes.
**Fillo draft:** *"One message every Sunday: what we're baking, where the van will be, and a photo of whatever didn't rise properly."*

### 5. The asterisk / parenthesis as the humour container
**Source:** Surreal — the celebrity-byline device; *"the perfect breakfast (or midnight snack)"*
**Mechanic:** Headline stays clean, appetising and scannable. Personality goes in the small text. Costs one line on mobile instead of the whole screen.
**Fillo drafts:**
- `Baked this morning.*` — *\*4:15am. We're still adjusting.*
- `Milk bread, 12-hour method.*` — *\*Mostly the dough resting. We sleep too.*

### 6. The one-word undercut
**Source:** Surreal — *"Maybe."* / *"Promise."* · WGAC — *"Ok, toot toot"*
**Mechanic:** A single trailing word after a claim, doing the reader's eye-roll for them. Pre-empts the smugness a quality claim otherwise triggers.
**Fillo drafts:** `The softest thing we make. Probably.` · `We'll never spam you. Promise.` · `Sold out in 40 minutes. Sorry. Thank you.`

### 7. Personality in the button label, action still intact
**Source:** Fishwife — `LMK When it's Back!`, `Pick your protein!`, `Get Started`
**Mechanic:** The warm label still contains the verb and the object. Test: cover the surrounding page — can you still tell what the button does?
**Fillo drafts:** `Add to the box` · `Tell me when it's back` · `Find the van` · `Pick your pickup time`
**Anti-pattern to avoid:** `Let's go!` or `Yes please!` — warm but action-free.

### 8. The empty state as an invitation, not a void
**Source:** Fishwife's *"New to Fishwife? Try The Starter Pack!"* pattern applied to zero-content moments; WGAC's *"Find your essentials"*
**Mechanic:** An empty cart / no-search-results / off-week screen should name the situation warmly and hand the user exactly one door.
**Fillo drafts:**
- Empty cart: *"Nothing in the box yet. This week we've got milk bread, matcha rolls and three kinds of cream pan."* → `See this week's bake`
- No results: *"We don't bake that — yet. Here's what's in the van this week."*
- Between drops: *"The van's off the road till Saturday. Kenji is proofing. Come back then, or get the Sunday message."*

### 9. The "how to eat this" block as a benefit, not an afterthought
**Source:** Fishwife — *"Enjoy tossed into a savory pasta, over warm rice with creamy avocado..."* · Graza — *"Great for everyday cooking like roasting, searing, poaching, pan frying, baking, and marinating."*
**Mechanic:** For an unfamiliar product, usage instruction *is* the persuasion. It removes the "I won't know what to do with it" objection that adjectives cannot touch.
**Fillo draft (on every PDF):** *"How to eat it — warm for 6 minutes at 160°C, or eat it cold from the bag on the way home. Tear it, don't slice it. Best on day one, still lovely on day two, French toast on day three."*

### 10. Confirmation copy that closes the loop like a person
**Source:** Liquid Death's *"Sucks. We can help you with that."* (acknowledge-then-act) · Graza's *"just shoot us a note to hello@graza.co and we will try to help"* (named human channel)
**Mechanic:** Order confirmation is the highest-emotion, lowest-designed screen in e-commerce. Name the next concrete thing that will happen, with a time, in a human voice.
**Fillo drafts:**
- Order placed: *"Got it. Your box is on Saturday's list. We'll message you Friday night with the exact spot and time — Kenji is usually five minutes early."*
- Waitlist joined: *"You're on the list. You'll hear from us before anyone else does."*
- Payment failed: *"That didn't go through — nothing's been charged. Try again, or write to us and we'll hold your box while you sort it."*

## Bonus: the three structural rules every brand here shares

Worth stating separately, because they are more transferable than any single line.

1. **Humour is placed where the user is bored, anxious, or disappointed** — FAQs, shipping, sold-out, email capture, error states. Never in the price, the guarantee, the return policy, or the checkout button. Liquid Death, Surreal and Graza all independently converge on this.
2. **The joke escorts the fact; it never replaces it.** Every funny line in this teardown that appears in a functional context still contains the number, the date, or the action.
3. **Personality that lives in *structure* (product names, nav labels, button labels, typography, IA) survives; personality sprinkled as adjectives does not.** Graza's Drizzle/Sizzle/Frizzle, Paper Boat's `memories` nav, Liquid Death's `Rest In Peach`, Fishwife's `LMK When it's Back!` — all structural. Compare Sleepy Owl, whose occasional "Smooooth AF" sits on an otherwise conventional site and reads as a costume rather than a character.
