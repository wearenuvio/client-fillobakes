# Fillo Bakes — Moodboard Refs: Community / Studio Case-Study Work

**Client:** Fillo Bakes — Japanese eggless moving-bakery van, Bengaluru.
**This lane:** what real agencies and designers have actually shipped for bakeries, Japanese F&B and delivery vehicles — to show the client the level, and the range of styles genuinely available to us.

## 1 — Japanese & Japanese-adjacent identity

**Black Star Pastry × Noritake / Studio Ongarato (Sydney)**
The closest single reference we have: a pastry brand borrowing Japanese illustration (Noritake's line-drawn characters) without costume-play, sitting on a strict black-and-white system that survives on a paper box. This is the "Japanese, not kitsch" tone Fillo needs.

**Suupaa / A Friend of Mine (Melbourne)**
A Japanese konbini built *outside* Japan: katakana-inflected signage, konbini colour logic, cheap-substrate packaging. Exactly Fillo's cultural-translation problem, solved by a non-Japanese studio. Caption it as Japanese-*inspired*, not native.
IMG: https://bpando.org/wp-content/uploads/11-Convience-Store-Branding-Signage-Suupa-Australia-A-Friend-of-Mine-BPO.jpg — *3000×2182*

**Tsukiyo / The Colour Club (UK)**
Japanese street food scaled to billboard. This is the test any van flank has to pass — does the wordmark still read at twenty metres and forty km/h? It does. Also Japanese-inspired, UK-made.

**% Arabica / Puddle (Kyoto)**
The minimalism benchmark and the only genuinely Japanese-domestic design in this section: one glyph, white plus timber, restraint as the entire brand. Sets the ceiling for how little Fillo actually needs to own.

**Lune Croissanterie / A Friend of Mine (Melbourne)**

## 4 — Indian studio F&B work

**Lucky Chan / NOW Media, Bengaluru**
The single closest analogue in the country: a Japanese F&B brand built for an Indian city. Steal the seigaiha wave band as a repeating device (it would work as a van skirt), the coral-on-indigo palette, and the flat-line maneki-neko that carries the Japanese signal without tipping into costume.
IMG: https://nowmedia.in/_astro/98a2ce1e761659e3bd106f499ed31f6c5c8a6b3c-4500x5625_Zn4fgR.webp — *1200×1500*
Source: https://nowmedia.in/work/lucky-chan

**Subko Coffee — Betabites (with Peter Cat Recording Co.), Mumbai**
Trilingual pack — Latin, Devanagari, Urdu on one face — plus an East-Asian illustration idiom rendered with Indian detail. The strongest proof that an Indian brand can borrow an Asian visual language and still read as legibly Indian. That is exactly Fillo's tightrope.
IMG: https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0308/0857/1963/files/Eclairs_by_Subko_Coffee_Roasters_-_A_collaboration_with_Peter_Cat_Recording_Company.jpg?v=1741161745 — *1500×1500*
Source: https://www.subko.coffee

**Blue Tokai drip bags / Lazy Eight Design**
India's biggest specialty identity rendered in a Japanese format — single-serve drip bag, near-monochrome pack, tiny mark, white-tile-and-wood styling. The India benchmark for restraint, and a reminder that the market here will pay for quiet.
IMG: https://framerusercontent.com/images/JhkdhKimnxYQWy2g2wlAwLEiDcs.png?width=2048&height=2048 — *2048×2048*
Source: https://lazyeight.design/portfolio/blue-tokai

**Licious carry bag / Codesign, Bengaluru**
One fat cursive logotype whose terminal doubles as a smile, on saturated magenta, with the mark blown up as an oversized tonal background. The blown-up-mark-as-surface trick transfers directly to the side of a van: one shape, readable at twenty metres.

## What's dominating bakery branding right now

**1. Warm kraft minimalism.** One wordmark in a tight neo-grotesque or a quiet transitional serif, a single spot colour, unbleached stock, beige-and-cream photography. Lune, Bon Bon, Antara 128, most of Blue Tokai. It is genuinely good and genuinely everywhere — which means it is now the *default*, and on a moving vehicle at street distance it collapses into a beige smudge. **Dodge this as the primary system**; keep it for the packaging layer only.

**2. Colour-block poster branding.** Saturated single-colour fields, oversized display type, the mark blown up as a surface rather than placed as a logo. Hank's Bagelry, Fuku, Oatier, Licious. Built for distance and for phone screens. **Ride this** — it is the only one of the three that solves the van problem.

**3. Drawn-motif tiling.** One line-drawn character or motif, repeated into a pattern that runs across every surface — box liner, wax paper, apron, wrap. Black Star × Noritake, Lucky Chan's maneki-neko, Suupaa. This is the fastest-growing of the three and it has the strongest natural affinity with Japanese graphic tradition. **Ride this too** — it is where Fillo's "Japanese" claim can live honestly without kitsch.

**The recommendation in one line:** colour-block livery (2) carrying a single drawn motif (3), with kraft minimalism (1) reserved for the bag and the box.

**Three keywords:** `KONBINI-BRIGHT` · `ONE-MOTIF-TILED` · `LEGIBLE-AT-SPEED`
