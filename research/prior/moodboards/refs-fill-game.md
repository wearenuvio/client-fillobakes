# Fillo Bakes — "Catch the Van" gap-fill refs

Direction: **the route as a game** — stamp rallies, gachapon, collect-them-all, the city as game board. Japanese eggless moving bakery, live GPS, Bengaluru.

This file fills five gap cells only. It deliberately does **not** repeat what the deck already has (Pokémon GO tram/PokéStop, eki-stamp booths + stamp books, gachapon walls, menko, sugoroku, Namma Metro map, Sanrio train wrap, Panini stickers, supermarket saver card).

**22 references. Every IMG url fetch-verified** — HTTP 200, real image bytes, ≥660px on the short edge (most ≥1400px). No Instagram CDN, no Pinterest. Sources are Behance's own CDN and Wikimedia Commons.

## 1. SOCIAL FEEDS — a feed built as a game board

**Hometown Scavenger Hunt — campaign identity (Rachel Aubel)**
The closest thing to Fillo that exists: a **van** in the logo, a dotted-route pattern as the brand texture, and printed "North Route / South Route" checklist maps people hold up and tick off. Proof that a route-hunt can carry a whole identity system, not just one post.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/max_1200_webp/a283ec170936645.6466364327633.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/170936645/Hometown-Scavenger-Hunt-Campaign-Design

**Cadbury CoCodes — Ogilvy Innovation Lab (South Africa)**
Every bar carries a **fragment** of a code; the app assembles fragments into a grid you fill in with friends. The exact mechanic for a Fillo loaf sticker that only completes when you've caught the van at several stops.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/02202e218343129.67a05fa58b0eb.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/218343129/Cadbury-CoCodes-Concept

**Hunt the District — Arena District, Columbus (Matt Cook)**
A scavenger hunt run **entirely inside the brand's own feed**: Watch (Tuesday post drops the items) → Track → Snap → Upload to the wall → weekly winners. Look past the dated 2011 chrome; the four-verb loop is the reference.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/hd_webp/4a5c9119708479.56040db325cfb.png
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/2623031/Hunt-the-District-Social-Media-Campaign

**Changi Airport × Ok Tested — Bingo Challenge (Deanne Fernandez)**
Branded content built as a bingo card two hosts race to complete inside one location. Useful for tone: the "challenge" framing lets a brand own a place without ever sounding like an ad.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/7b2fe8251832201.6a42e4cb259ae.png
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/251832201/Changi-Airport-Ok-Tested-Bingo-Challenge

## 2. PACKAGING — collectible packaging systems, food + omake

**MILO "Săn thẻ hình dán" collectible card hunt (Nestlé Vietnam)**
Cards clipped to the multipack itself, characters as the reward, the whole thing announced with a live "24H" news-ticker key visual. Pack **is** the game piece — the single most transferable idea for a Fillo loaf sleeve.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/4ad4ab176022503.64be2ff93abcc.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/176022503/MILO-Multiverse-Collectible-Cards_Social-AWO-Campaign

**Totoy — Pasada Series blind box, flat dieline (Faith Mata)**
Shows exactly where the collect-them-all work happens on a small box: hero window on the front, the **full three-figure line-up printed on the back panel** so you can see what you're missing. Copy this panel logic onto a bakery box.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/2800_webp/2210ec240739767.6985cf1b61356.png
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/240739767/Totoy-Pasada-Series-Blind-Box

**Sideline Mates Wind-Up Series — numbered packs (Alvin Szeto)**
"FIGURE NO.1 / NO.2 / NO.3" set in a banner across the top of each box, one country per pack. Numbering the pack is the cheapest possible collectibility signal — no new artwork, just an index.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/4ffc9e38055141.5754c74305caa.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/38055141/Collectable-Toy-Series-Sideline-Mates-Wind-Up-Series

**Bikkuriman "Devil vs Angel" sticker — layout anatomy (Lotte)**
A schematic of the most successful food-sticker system ever made: front = name + illustration; back = name, description text, small inset illustration, closing line, and a **serial number**. A ready-made template for a Fillo sticker back.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b4/%E3%83%93%E3%83%83%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AA%E3%83%9E%E3%83%B3%E6%82%AA%E9%AD%94VS%E5%A4%A9%E4%BD%BF%E3%82%B7%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AB_%E5%9F%BA%E6%9C%AC%E3%83%AC%E3%82%A4%E3%82%A2%E3%82%A6%E3%83%88.svg/1920px-%E3%83%93%E3%83%83%E3%82%AF%E3%83%AA%E3%83%9E%E3%83%B3%E6%82%AA%E9%AD%94VS%E5%A4%A9%E4%BD%BF%E3%82%B7%E3%83%BC%E3%83%AB_%E5%9F%BA%E6%9C%AC%E3%83%AC%E3%82%A4%E3%82%A2%E3%82%A6%E3%83%88.svg.png

**Kinder Surprise, halved — the toy premium inside the food**
The Western cousin of Glico's caramel-with-a-toy *omake*: the capsule is structurally part of the product, not an add-on. Argues for a Fillo pack where the surprise sits *inside* rather than stuck on the outside.

## 3. VAN / VEHICLE — playful wrapped vehicles

**Oscar Mayer Wienermobile — Meat Up in Miami, 2022**
The benchmark for "the vehicle *is* the logo". No wrap graphics at all: the silhouette does the branding, so it reads from 200 metres and photographs itself.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Oscar_Mayer_Wienermobile_-_Meat_Up_in_Miami_2022.jpg/1920px-Oscar_Mayer_Wienermobile_-_Meat_Up_in_Miami_2022.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oscar_Mayer_Wienermobile_-_Meat_Up_in_Miami_2022.jpg

**Wienermobile parked on Natchez Street, New Orleans, 2021**
The same object in ordinary street context — kerbside, in traffic, among parked cars. This is the honest test of how a novelty vehicle actually behaves on a Bengaluru road, not on a show stand.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/97/Wienermobile_on_Natchez_Street%2C_New_Orleans%2C_July_2021.jpg/1920px-Wienermobile_on_Natchez_Street%2C_New_Orleans%2C_July_2021.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Wienermobile_on_Natchez_Street,_New_Orleans,_July_2021.jpg

**Big Idaho Potato Truck**
A single absurd object on a flatbed, with the brand line running down the trailer skirt. Shows the cheaper route to a Wienermobile: keep a normal chassis, mount one oversized hero prop.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/8d/Big_Idaho_Potato_truck.jpg/1920px-Big_Idaho_Potato_truck.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Big_Idaho_Potato_truck.jpg

**Dragon Ball *itasha* — 4th International Auto Show, Bengaluru, 2025**
A full anime-graphic wrap carried onto doors, bonnet and even the seat covers — photographed **in Bengaluru**. Local proof that the itasha language reads here, and a warning about how loud a full-bleed character wrap gets.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/4th_International_Auto_Show%2C_Bangalore_%282025%29_213.jpg/1920px-4th_International_Auto_Show%2C_Bangalore_%282025%29_213.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:4th_International_Auto_Show,_Bangalore_(2025)_213.jpg

**Itasha limousine, Akihabara**
The Japanese original, at street level in the district the whole moodboard borrows from — a long vehicle treated as one continuous illustrated panel. Direct model for a Fillo van side read as a single scene rather than a logo lockup.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/85/AKIBA%EF%BD%9E%E3%83%AA%E3%83%A0%E3%82%B8%E3%83%B3%E7%97%9B%E8%BB%8A%EF%BD%9E_%2810062850856%29.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AKIBA%EF%BD%9E%E3%83%AA%E3%83%A0%E3%82%B8%E3%83%B3%E7%97%9B%E8%BB%8A%EF%BD%9E_(10062850856).jpg

## 4. STREET — physical game furniture

**Girls playing marelle (hopscotch), Jaura, India**
The grid scratched straight onto Indian ground, no equipment, no permission. The lowest-cost "game board" Fillo can put on a pavement at a stop — and it already belongs here.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1d/Filles_jouant_%C3%A0_la_marelle%2C_Jaura%2C_Inde.jpg/1920px-Filles_jouant_%C3%A0_la_marelle%2C_Jaura%2C_Inde.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Filles_jouant_%C3%A0_la_marelle,_Jaura,_Inde.jpg

**GiGO game arcade, Akihabara — full-height facade**
An entire building elevation used as one stacked, numbered, floor-by-floor sign. The reference for how loud a game frontage is allowed to be, and how the vertical stack organises it.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/db/GiGo_Gaming_Arcade%2C_Akihabara_%2853416976669%29.jpg/1920px-GiGo_Gaming_Arcade%2C_Akihabara_%2853416976669%29.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:GiGo_Gaming_Arcade,_Akihabara_(53416976669).jpg

**Super Potato retro game shop, Akihabara**
The opposite move: hand-lettered, pixel-era shopfront where the *collection* is the display. Model for a van hatch wall where every SKU and every stamp is visible at once.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/c2/Akihabara_-_Super_Potato_Retro_Game_Shop.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Akihabara_-_Super_Potato_Retro_Game_Shop.jpg

**Fenway Park's manual scoreboard, from inside the Green Monster**
A leaderboard operated by hand, one slotted plate at a time. Argues hard for a Fillo "today's stops / today's sold out" board that a human changes, rather than a screen — analogue scoreboards are photographed, screens are not.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9c/Checking_out_the_manual_scoreboard_in_the_belly_of_the_Monster.jpg/1920px-Checking_out_the_manual_scoreboard_in_the_belly_of_the_Monster.jpg
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Checking_out_the_manual_scoreboard_in_the_belly_of_the_Monster.jpg

## 5. SCREENS — playful map & quest UI

**AR Scavenger Hunt — home / quest screen (Dmytro Tatiievskyi)**
Points, level bar, "2 challenges to next reward", a running **Summer Hunt 1/4** card, and nearest challenges by walking time — with Map and AR Scan sitting in the tab bar. This is a live-GPS food brand's app, already designed.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/717b68214543047.6759c79662cbb.png
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/214543047/AR-Scavenger-Hunt-A-Location-Based-Mobile-App

**AR Scavenger Hunt — leaderboard, achievements grid, hunt detail**
The achievements screen is the key frame: **36/140 unlocked**, the rest greyed "?" slots in a grid. That empty-slot grid is precisely how a Fillo stamp rally should look before you've caught the van.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400_webp/16cf25214543047.6759c7965f45f.png
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/214543047/AR-Scavenger-Hunt-A-Location-Based-Mobile-App

**Porsche gamification app (Lukas Hofstætter)**
A restrained, premium take: a plain activity feed of earned events ("visiting the Geneva show earns you 50 km", "you completed the Le Mans Challenge") with three point dials underneath. Proof that a points system can stay grown-up.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/37153661372403.5a6c4976b84fd.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/61372403/Porsche-Gamification-App-UI

**Tele2 loyalty — collectible robot upgrade ladder (Olga Mikerova)**
One character evolving across Steel → Bronze → Silver → Gold → Platinum tiers, each rendered as a distinct collectible object. The alternative to badges: **one mascot, many states**, which suits a single-mascot bakery far better.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400_webp/2f2c88157323711.69f14ac949c40.jpg
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/157323711/Gamification-System-for-Tele2-Loyalty-Program
