## Stories (final copy)

### Maneki Pop
In Japan, the cat in the window beckons customers in. Fillo has no window. It has wheels.
So the beckoning travels: a face on the van, a name children shout at the gate, a character whose whole job is to show what fuwa fuwa feels like before you taste it.
Bengaluru has already fallen for one lucky character. The next one delivers bread.

### Shōwa Press
Fillo's founders tell of the milk vendor's bell, bread reaching the doorstep like a small daily ceremony.
Japan kept that same era in ink: brush-lettered shopfronts, paper labels, a stamp that says a person made this.
This direction invents no heritage. It borrows two honest ones — Bengaluru's doorstep mornings and Japan's 150 years of bakery craft — and prints them on every surface.

### Catch the Van
Every brand fights for attention. Almost none earn pursuit.
Fillo's van already broadcasts its position every fifteen seconds — the machinery of a city-wide game wearing the costume of a delivery feature.
Japan has played this for a century: station stamps, capsule prizes, routes drawn as board games. Make the bread the prize, and Bengaluru becomes the board.
