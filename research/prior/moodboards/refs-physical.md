**Antique canvas distillery work apron (Nationaal Jenevermuseum)** — Undyed heavy canvas, gone soft and stained with use, simple cord ties at the neck; the material and patina to aim for rather than a crisp branded bib. — IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/ea/Canvas_schort_distilleerderij%2C_objectnr_27264.JPG — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Canvas_schort_distilleerderij,_objectnr_27264.JPG

## 3. A-frames, standees & street signage with charm

**Chubokuchaya teahouse, dusk** — Wooden kiosk ringed with A-frames, hanging paper lanterns, hand-brushed menu panels and a red bench for waiting; this is the emotional target for what a Fillo stop should feel like when it's set up. — IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/Chubokuchaya_2018-03-14.jpeg — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Chubokuchaya_2018-03-14.jpeg

**「本日の品切れ / ただいま頑張って準備中!!」A-frame** — White-painted wooden A-frame, chalk brush lettering, and a small paper card taped to the corner listing today's sold-out items with the next service time (17:30) chalked in. The sold-out card is a ritual worth stealing outright. — IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/6d/%E6%9C%AC%E6%97%A5%E3%81%AE_%E5%93%81%E5%88%87%E3%82%8C_%285059803744%29.jpg — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:本日の_品切れ_(5059803744).jpg

**Hand-lettered street A-board under a red lantern (暖簾 II)** — Vertical red-and-black brush columns on a white board, set on a narrow pavement with a bus passing; proof that a dense hand-lettered board reads at speed and beats any printed panel for warmth. — IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5a/%E6%9A%96%E7%B0%BE_II_%2814764210132%29.jpg — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:暖簾_II_(14764210132).jpg

**Kappogi izakaya shopfront, Shibata** — Illuminated brush-lettered kanban above the door, white noren in the entry, a row of white nobori banners, and a printed standee at the kerb; the full vertical signage stack a small shop deploys, and a study in how the standee sits without looking corporate. — IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/97/Kappogi_Shibata_store.JPG — https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Kappogi_Shibata_store.JPG

## Palette notes

Pulled from the refs above, in rough order of how much surface each should occupy.

- **Undyed canvas / oatmeal** (`#D9CDB8` → `#EFE7D8`) — aprons, awnings, noren, the stall cloth. The base. Should read as "washed a hundred times," never bright white.
- **Warm plywood & cedar** (`#C08B4E` → `#8A5A2B`) — counter ledges, crate faces, menu-board frames, the A-frame timber. The second-largest surface and what makes the whole kit feel built rather than bought.
- **Chalkboard ink** (`#22252A`, near-black with a green-grey cast) + **chalk white** (`#F2F0EA`) — every hand-lettered board. The high-contrast pair that carries all the daily-changing information.
- **Lantern vermilion** (`#D8412F`) — the single accent, used sparingly: lantern, awning stripe, one stroke in the brush lettering, the bell pull. Appears in the teahouse, Borough Market and A-board refs alike. Never more than ~5% of any frame.
- **Brass / warm gold** (`#B08A3E`) — the bell, apron hardware, hooks and rings. Small, tactile, ages well.
- **Indigo & noren blue** (`#2E4763`) — apron straps, banner grounds, crate blue. The cool note that keeps the warm palette from going nostalgic-sepia.
- **Galvanised steel grey** (`#9AA0A3`) — milk can, van hatch, crate hardware. The utility register; earns the "this thing works" read.
- **Varanasi ochre** (`#C2622A`) — the India nostalgia note. Best used as a photographic backdrop tone rather than a graphic fill.

Practical rule the refs keep repeating: **warm neutral ground, one red, one brass, everything else earned by materials.** Signage does the shouting; the kit stays quiet.
