## 1. Japanese premium shokupan bags — the "gift-grade white bag"

**Nogami (乃が美) — storefront, white bags stacked at the counter**
The reference point for Fillo: Nogami's *nama shokupan* is famously **eggless**, and it is sold in a plain white paper bag with nothing but a brushed logotype. Proof that eggless + premium + minimal is already the Japanese luxury code, not a compromise.

## Palette notes

- **Base / non-negotiable:** paper white and unbleached off-white (#FFFFFF → #F3EFE7). Both Nogami and Ginza Nishikawa carry the entire premium claim on *uncoated white with one mark on it*. This should be Fillo's default bag.
- **Warm neutral field:** kraft and oat (#C9A87C, #E3D5BF, #D8CBB4) for sleeves, insert cards and inner trays — the ANKO AUN / KOTORI zone. Reads handmade without reading rustic-cliché.
- **One deep anchor:** an indigo-to-near-black (#1B2A4A, #14171C) for the wordmark, tamper tape and box interiors. Dorayaki and AOKI both prove a single dark carries print cost down and contrast up.
- **One warm accent, used sparingly:** saffron/marigold (#E8A33D, #F2C14E) or the Bombay Sweet Shop terracotta-red (#C8452F). This is the Bengaluru half of the brief — it should appear on the seal and the band, never on the bag body.
- **Metallic, once only:** a matte gold or brass foil restricted to the circular seal (Anand Sweets, KOTORI). One foil hit per pack, maximum.
- **Green as a signature, optional:** KOTORI's deep pine (#2C3A2E) with gold reads ceremonial and Japanese without matcha-cliché — worth testing for the festive/gift SKU.
- **Avoid:** full-bleed pastel, transparent-window plastic as the hero material, and more than three inks on the primary bag. Every ref here that works is one substrate + one mark + one closure.
