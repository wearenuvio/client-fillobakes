# Fillo Bakes — Moodboard Refs: JAPANESE RETRO / SHOPFRONT / PRINT-SHOP

Direction: Shōwa-era nostalgia, noren curtains, hand-painted shop signage, kissaten graphics,
red-ink hanko stamps, vintage Japanese bread/milk packaging, mingei/woodblock-adjacent lettering,
retro matchbox labels. Every `IMG:` URL below was fetched and confirmed to return a real
image file (jpg/png) at the stated pixel size.

---

## A. REAL SHOPFRONTS — the ones that actually earned the nostalgia

### 1. Ginza Kimuraya Sōhonten (est. 1869, inventor of anpan)
The single most load-bearing reference: a bread shop that has survived 150+ years on one
hand-carved vertical signboard and a fixed cream/gold/black palette. Fillo's "old shop, new van"
story lives here.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/23/Ginza_Kimuraya_Sohonten_2022-11-16.jpg (4031×3024)
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/9/92/%E9%8A%80%E5%BA%A7%E6%9C%A8%E6%9D%91%E5%AE%B6_%2816261125541%29.jpg (3072×4608, lit night facade)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ginza_Kimuraya_Sohonten_2022-11-16.jpg · https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:銀座木村家_(16261125541).jpg

### 2. Kimuraya sakura-anpan — the salted cherry blossom pressed into the crown
An edible hanko. Literal proof that a single stamped mark on a plain bun is enough branding.
Direct model for a Fillo "pressed mark" on every eggless bun.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8f/Ginza-Kimuraya-Sakura-Anpan---2023-11-28_001.jpg (3648×2736)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ginza-Kimuraya-Sakura-Anpan---2023-11-28_001.jpg

### 3. Kimuraya official brand photography — how a 150-year-old shop shoots itself today
Warm paper ground, centred product, restrained kanji lockup. This is the "modern-retro but not
costume" tone target.
IMG: https://www.kimuraya-sohonten.co.jp/cms/kimuraya/img/top/main_02.jpg (1000×1000)
Source: https://www.kimuraya-sohonten.co.jp/

### 4. Pelican Bakery / Pelican Cafe, Asakusa (est. 1942)
A bakery that makes only shokupan and rolls and has never changed its wordmark. Extreme menu
discipline expressed as extreme graphic discipline — the cleanest argument for Fillo staying
single-idea.
IMG: https://pelicancafe.jp/img/ogp_c.jpg (1200×630)
Source: https://pelicancafe.jp/

### 5. Fukuda Pan, Morioka — the regional koppe-pan counter
Utilitarian tiled shopfront, fat hand-lettered kana, no styling. This is the *van-adjacent*,
working-class register of Japanese bakery — not the boutique register.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/8a/Fukuda_Pan_Nagatacho_Main_Store.jpg (3776×2520)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fukuda_Pan_Nagatacho_Main_Store.jpg

### 6. Nakano Shōtengai — covered arcade signage density
Hand-painted vertical banners, stacked kana, competing reds and yellows. The texture Fillo's
van should feel like it drove out of.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/37/Sh%C5%8Dtengai_of_Nakano_Interior.jpg (5183×3887)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Shōtengai_of_Nakano_Interior.jpg

## B. KISSATEN — retro coffee-shop graphic register

### 7. Tsubakiya Coffee, Ginza 7-chōme
Dark wood, gold-leaf lettering, brass and glass. The "expensive nostalgia" end of kissaten —
useful as the ceiling for Fillo's signage.

Amber light, aged cream walls, mismatched signage. The colour temperature reference for the

## C. PRINT-SHOP / STAMP / WRAPPING

### 12. "Japanese Bakery" — J.M.W. Silver engraving, 1867
Pre-photographic line engraving of a Japanese baking scene. Mingei/woodblock-adjacent mark-making
that can be redrawn as a Fillo illustration system (van, oven, hands).
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d2/Japanese_bakery-J._M._W._Silver.jpg (928×590)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Japanese_bakery-J._M._W._Silver.jpg

### 13. Japanese matchbox labels, 1920s–40s (incl. a bakery label)
Two-to-three-colour lithography on cheap stock, tiny lockups, vermilion + cream + black.
The exact scale and economy Fillo needs for stickers, bag seals and van decals.
Note: these are archive originals at native 500px — sized for stamp/sticker mocks, not hero use.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/b/b7/Matchbox_label_-_Bakery_-_Japan_%28circa_1940s%29_-_MBP1613668162.jpg (500×321, bakery label)
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/e/eb/Matchbox_label_-_Red_Raincoats_-_Japan_%28circa_1920s%29_-_MBP1510740760.jpg (500×321)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Matchbox_labels_from_Japan

### 14. Personalised hanko / digital stamp studies — Kazumi Haseyama
Red-ink seal construction: how a name is compressed into a circle or square and still reads.
Direct template for a Fillo hanko mark stamped on bags, receipts and the van door.
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/hd/3c8308224052679.6804534e707a5.jpg (1240×745)
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/224052679/Personalized-Japanese-Hanko-Design-Digital-Stamp-Art

### 15. Noren — shop curtains as the brand's front door
The van's awning/side-curtain analogue: cloth, one colour, one mark, split panels you walk through.
Includes a museum textile piece for crest/mon lettering construction.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/8/81/Noren_of_a_yakitori_restaurant_by_MShades_in_Kyoto.jpg (2816×2112)
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cf/Shop_curtain_of_Maniwa_City_Katsuyama.JPG (1967×1420, indigo)
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/26/Brooklyn_Museum_-_Noren_%28Shop_Curtain%29.jpg (768×668, museum piece)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Noren

### 16. Furoshiki — cloth wrapping as ritual, not packaging
For a moving van that can't carry rigid boxes: the wrap *is* the pack. Repeat-pattern cloth
doubles as the wrapping-paper system.
IMG: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/2b/Furoshiki%2C_traditional_japanese_wrapping_technique_with_cloths_%281%29.jpg (5184×2920)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Furoshiki

### 17. 掬菓 argyi — wagashi identity, Timeroots Design (本時制作)
Traditional-confectionery identity built on paper stock, seal-red accents, letterpress-weight
type and a restrained grid. Closest existing execution to "heritage sweet shop, made now".
IMG: https://mir-s3-cdn-cf.behance.net/project_modules/1400/cb46b6167688181.642d568f61d69.jpg (1400×881)
Source: https://www.behance.net/gallery/167688181/argyi

The glass bottle and the printed circular paper cap. Fillo's milk-vendor origin story has a

A roadside milk dispenser wearing full brand livery. The nearest historical object to "a small

How Japan merchandises a soft, fragile, photogenic bread product at counter scale: clear film,
tiny label, price card, repetition. Fillo's display logic, solved.

### 21. Onigiri Yagura — Brand & Packaging, Naomi Fujii
A Japanese designer doing modern-retro Japanese food packaging: seal-red mark, kraft/cream
ground, vertical kana, tower motif. The cleanest packaging benchmark in the whole lane.

### 22. Sando, Japanese Sandwiches — Muse Studio · Katsu Sando — plai büro
