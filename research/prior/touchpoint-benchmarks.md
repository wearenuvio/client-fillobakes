# Consumer Touchpoint Effectiveness Benchmarks — Fillo Bakes

**Purpose:** hard benchmark data to underpin a consumer-touchpoint prioritisation matrix for Fillo Bakes (Japanese eggless bakery, moving bakery van, Bengaluru, building D2C).

**Compiled:** August 2026. All figures carry source + year. Geography tagged `[India]` or `[Global]`.

### How to read the confidence tags

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **High** | Peer-reviewed study, large-N platform dataset, or established research house (Nielsen, Ipsos, Klaviyo, HBS, Google). Methodology visible. |
| **Med** | Credible industry aggregator or vendor with disclosed dataset, but no published methodology; or a widely-replicated figure whose original source is traceable. |
| **Low** | Vendor/marketing-blog claim, circular citation, no traceable primary study, or a rate-card estimate. Use directionally only — do not quote externally. |

> **Provenance warning applied throughout:** several categories (vehicle wraps, WhatsApp, UGC) are dominated by vendors selling the service. Those numbers are marked Low and should be treated as ceilings, not expectations.

## 1. Vehicle branding / van livery as OOH

This is Fillo's single most distinctive owned asset, and it also has the weakest evidence base. Read the India rate-card data (which is checkable) with more weight than the US recall claims (which are not).

### Impressions and reach

- **A mobile branding van in India generates 80,000–1,50,000 impressions/day** at an average **₹15–30 CPM**. *(Vigyapan Mart, transit advertising in India, 2026)* `[India]` — **Med**
- **Bengaluru-specific:** a single branded van covering **100–150 km/day** delivers **2–3.5 lakh daily impressions**; a 7-day single-van campaign delivers **15–25 lakh total impressions at ₹0.50–1.20 CPM**. *(The Mediaverse, Mobile Van Branding Bangalore, 2026)* `[India]` — **Low** *(vendor rate card; impression counts are modelled, not measured — the two India CPM figures differ by ~20x, which tells you how soft the modelling is)*
- **Bengaluru rate card:** L-shape van ₹12,000/day (7-day min), T-shape ₹18,000/day, LED van ₹25,000/day, +18% GST; 15–20% discount on monthly packages. *(The Mediaverse, 2026)* `[India]` — **Med** *(published rate card — useful as a real cost anchor even if impressions are estimated)*
- **US benchmark for comparison:** full vehicle wraps generate **30,000–70,000 impressions/day**, or **400–600 impressions per mile**. *(Widely cited across wrap-industry sources incl. Wrapmate, Chicago Fleet Wraps, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(no traceable primary methodology)*

### Cost per thousand vs other media

- **Vehicle wrap CPM $0.48 — the lowest of any OOH format.** Comparison set from the same 3M-attributed table: billboards $3.56, radio $7.75, newspaper $19.70, magazine $21.46, TV $23.70. *(Attributed to 3M; republished by LookUpAPlate, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(3M original study is not publicly retrievable; the figure circulates without a date)*
- **India transit advertising CPM ₹50–150** — described as cheaper than most other OOH methods. *(Vigyapan Mart, 2026)* `[India]` — **Med**

### Recall and noticing

- **64% of US residents aged 16+ noticed a wrapped vehicle in the previous month; 44% noticed one in the previous week.** Wrapped vehicles ranked as the **most-noticed moving OOH format** and second most-noticed outdoor format overall. *(Nielsen / OAAA OOH study, 2019)* `[Global]` — **High** *(strongest single stat in this category — Nielsen methodology, real sample)*
- **More than 90% of US travellers noticed OOH advertising in the past month; 80% in the past week.** *(Nielsen / OAAA, 2019)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **"97% message recall for vehicle marketing vs 19% for stationary ads."** Attributed to 3M. *(Republished by Chicago Fleet Wraps, LookUpAPlate, and dozens of wrap vendors, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** — ⚠️ **Do not use externally.** This is the most-repeated number in the category and the original 3M study cannot be located. Treat as vendor folklore.
- **78% recall after 30 days for full wraps; 65% of pedestrians notice wraps; multi-vehicle fleets amplify recall ~3x.** *(Chicago Fleet Wraps, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(estimate, no methodology)*

### Food-truck / van-specific effectiveness

- **FMCG sampling case, Bengaluru:** 1 L-shape van × 30 days distributed **40,000+ product samples at a stated 15% trial-to-purchase conversion.** *(The Mediaverse case study, 2026)* `[India]` — **Low** *(single vendor-reported case, unaudited — but it is the closest available analogue to Fillo's actual van+sampling model, so worth noting as a directional hypothesis)*
- No credible food-truck-specific branding effectiveness study was found in this search. **Gap.**

**Read-through for Fillo:** the van is defensible as a low-CPM awareness asset, and the Nielsen finding that wrapped vehicles are the most-noticed *moving* OOH format is real. But the van's measurable value is far more likely to come from the sampling/trial interaction at the stop (section 10) than from drive-by impressions. Budget it as a trial-generation asset with awareness as a free by-product, not as a billboard.

### India festival gifting

**Read-through for Fillo:** packaging is unusually well-evidenced as a *retention* lever (61% repurchase intent, 60% negative repurchase risk) rather than an acquisition lever. Combined with the Diwali gifting data, a giftable festive SKU is the best-supported single product bet in this document.

## 3. WhatsApp (India)

⚠️ **Category-wide caveat.** Almost every number here originates from a company selling WhatsApp Business API services. The 98% open rate in particular is repeated so universally that its origin is untraceable. Also note a definitional problem: WhatsApp "open" means the message was viewed in a list where all messages are visible, which is not comparable to an email open pixel. **Treat WhatsApp-vs-email open-rate comparisons as structurally misleading; compare on CTR and conversion instead.**

### Open rates and CTR vs email

- **WhatsApp Business message open rate ~98% vs ~22–25% for email in India.** *(Repeated across WhatSender, Interakt, CampaignHQ and others, 2025–2026)* `[India]` — **Low** *(untraceable origin; see caveat above)*
- **Interakt platform data (2024–25):** average open rate **96%** across SMB users, top decile **99%**; average campaign **CTR 26%**, top decile **35%**; average **conversion rate 12%**, top decile **18%**. *(Interakt WhatsApp Business API Usage Benchmarks, 2025)* `[India]` — **Med** *(actual platform aggregate from an India-focused provider — the best-provenanced WhatsApp data found, though sample size undisclosed)*
- **95% of WhatsApp messages in India are opened within 3 minutes;** ~80% read within five minutes. *(Vendor aggregators, 2025–2026)* `[India]` — **Low**
- **WhatsApp broadcast CTR 5–15%** on realistic campaigns *(Sinch, 2026)* vs vendor claims of **15–80%** *(Sendwo, 2025)*. `[Global]` — **Low** — the 20-fold spread across vendor sources is itself the finding. Plan against the low end.
- **Email CTR benchmark for comparison: ~2–5%.** *(Multiple, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Conversion and commerce

- **WhatsApp e-commerce broadcast conversion 3–7%, vs under 1% for email.** *(Vendor aggregators, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(most plausible conversion figure in this set — use this rather than the 45–60% claims)*
- **"WhatsApp conversational commerce conversion 45–60%, up to 12x traditional channels."** *(Vendor aggregators, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low** — ⚠️ implausible as a campaign-level rate; likely measures conversion *within an already-initiated buying conversation*. Do not plan against.
- **Cart recovery conversion via WhatsApp: 18–25%.** *(Interakt platform data, 2025)* `[India]` — **Med** *(compare with 3.33% for email cart flows in section 4 — even discounted heavily, WhatsApp cart recovery looks materially stronger)*
- **20% higher repeat purchase rates with automated workflows; 27% increase in conversion probability with automated 24-hour follow-ups.** *(Interakt, 2025)* `[India]` — **Med**
- **India WhatsApp commerce GMV reached ₹2.5 lakh crore in 2025.** *(Vendor aggregator, 2025)* `[India]` — **Low**
- **65% UPI adoption among WhatsApp users in India, 98.1% transaction success rate.** *(Vendor aggregator, 2025)* `[India]` — **Low**

### Channels vs broadcast lists

- **WhatsApp Channels reached 500M+ monthly active users within one year of its September 2023 launch. India is the #1 market.** *(Meta / platform reporting, 2024–2025)* `[Global/India]` — **High** *(Meta-published adoption figure)*
- **India has 535M+ WhatsApp users — the largest market globally.** *(Statista / platform reporting, 2025)* `[India]` — **High**
- **Structural differences** *(Sinch, Infobip, 2026)* `[Global]` — **High** *(product documentation, not a claim)*:
  - **Broadcast list:** capped at **256 saved contacts**, private, recipients must have your number saved, **replies open a 1:1 conversation** — supports sales and support.
  - **Channel:** **unlimited followers**, public and discoverable, **one-way only** (emoji reactions only), chronological feed with **no algorithmic suppression — every follower sees every post**. Brand cannot see who followers are.

**Read-through for Fillo:** Channels are the right awareness/announcement surface (unlimited, discoverable, no reach throttling — a genuinely rare property in 2026). Broadcast/API is the right ordering and retention surface because replies convert. Run both; they do different jobs. Plan against 3–7% broadcast conversion, not the 45–60% vendor claims.

## 4. Email lifecycle

Best-evidenced category in this document — Klaviyo publishes large-N aggregates with disclosed sample sizes.

### Welcome flow

- **Welcome series first-email open rates 45–65%,** declining to 30–40% by email 5. *(Aggregated Klaviyo agency benchmarks, 2025–2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Welcome flow conversion benchmark 12–18%** *(agency benchmark, 2026)* vs **4.37% observed across a 14-brand real-data sample** *(BS&Co Klaviyo flow benchmarks, 2026)*. `[Global]` — **Med** — the gap between aspirational benchmark and observed reality is instructive; **plan against ~4–5%**.
- **Welcome series average RPR $2.65.** *(Klaviyo agency aggregates, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Post-purchase flow

- **Flows generate ~41% of total email revenue from just 5.3% of sends** — RPR nearly **18x higher than campaigns**. *(Klaviyo benchmark data, 2025–2026)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **A 7-email post-purchase sequence drives 18–24% of email revenue** for DTC brands. *(Top Growth Marketing, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Post-purchase messages see 217% higher open rates and 90% higher revenue per recipient than broadcast campaigns.** *(Top Growth Marketing, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Top-performing ecommerce brands generate 30–40% of total revenue from email + SMS combined.** *(Multiple agency aggregates, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Abandoned cart

Primary Klaviyo dataset: **143,000+ abandoned cart flows sent in 2023.** — **High** confidence throughout.

- **Open rate 50.5% average / 65.34% top decile**
- **Click rate 6.25% average / 13.33% top decile**
- **Conversion (placed-order) rate 3.33% average / 7.69% top decile**
- **Revenue per recipient $3.65 average / $28.89 top decile**
- **Food & Beverage specifically outperforms: 52.16% open, 6.63% click, 3.66% conversion** — above average on all three.
- *(Klaviyo Abandoned Cart Benchmark Report, 2023 data)* `[Global]`
- Abandoned cart flows drive **the highest average RPR and highest conversion rate of all flow types**. *(Klaviyo, 2023)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Cart recovery rate benchmark 8–12% (P90).** *(Attribuly, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

**Read-through for Fillo:** F&B beats the cross-industry average on every abandoned-cart metric — this flow is the highest-ROI email build. But note section 3: WhatsApp cart recovery at 18–25% (Med confidence) may outperform email's 3.66% (High confidence) by enough to justify WhatsApp-first cart recovery in India.

## 5. Google Business Profile + reviews

### Local discovery share

- **42% of searchers click on Google map pack results for local queries.** *(Backlinko, 2024, via BrightLocal)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **One in five consumers conduct local searches directly within maps apps** (Google, Apple, Bing). *(BrightLocal Consumer Search Behavior, 2025)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Average SMB receives 1,009 searches/month through GBP** — 781 discovery views and 228 direct branded views. *(BiziQ aggregation of GBP data, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Review reading

- **97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses.** *(BrightLocal Local Consumer Review Survey, 2026)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **71% of consumers use Google specifically to read local business reviews.** *(BrightLocal, 2026)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **65% of consumers have rejected a business for review-related reasons** — negative reviews, low rating, or no/outdated reviews. *(BrightLocal Consumer Search Behavior, 2026)* `[Global]` — **High** — note "no reviews" counts as a rejection trigger, which matters for a new brand.
- **Google hosts 73% of all online business reviews** (Yelp 6%, Facebook 3%, Tripadvisor 3%). *(BrightLocal, 2025)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **83% of consumers read Google reviews before deciding.** *(BrightLocal Consumer Review Survey, 2025)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **India-specific review-reading rate for food: not found.** Nearest available: LocalCircles found **over two-thirds of Indian consumers ordering via food-delivery apps are more concerned about quality than delivery speed** (n=63,000+ across 307 districts; 44% tier-1, 33% tier-2, 23% tier-3/4/rural). *(LocalCircles, 2019)* `[India]` — **Med** *(large sample, but a proxy — it evidences quality-sensitivity, not review-reading)*. **Gap.**

### Conversion lift per star

- **A one-star rating increase on Yelp drives a 5–9% increase in restaurant revenue.** Effect is **driven entirely by independent restaurants** — chain-affiliated restaurants show no effect. Study used a regression-discontinuity design exploiting Yelp's rating rounding (3.24 → 3 stars vs 3.25 → 3.5 stars) to control for actual quality. Analysed all Seattle restaurants 2003–2009 against Washington State Department of Revenue data. *(Michael Luca, "Reviews, Reputation, and Revenue: The Case of Yelp.com," Harvard Business School Working Paper, 2011)* `[Global]` — **High** — ⭐ **strongest causal evidence in this entire document, and it applies specifically to independents like Fillo.**

### Photos on GBP

- **Businesses with photos receive 42% more requests for driving directions and 35% more click-throughs to their website** than businesses without. *(Google, own platform data)* `[Global]` — **High** *(Google-published; date not stated, in circulation since ~2018)*
- **Complete profiles: customers 2.7x more likely to consider the business reputable, 70% more likely to visit, 50% more likely to purchase.** *(Google, via BrightLocal)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Complete GBP profiles earn 7x more clicks and 70% more in-store visits than incomplete profiles.** *(BiziQ, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Listings with 10+ photos get up to 2x more engagement; profiles refreshed every 90 days earn ~22% more engagement.** *(Vendor aggregation, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Low**

**Read-through for Fillo:** GBP is the highest-confidence, lowest-cost touchpoint in this document. The Luca finding gives a causal, independent-restaurant-specific revenue number that almost nothing else here can match. A moving van complicates GBP (no fixed address) — worth resolving whether to register a fixed production address as the primary listing.

- **41% of diners have used social media to research which restaurant to visit — rising to 67% of Gen Z and 57% of Millennials.** *(Restaurant social aggregation, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**

- **Reels average reach rate 37.87% in 2024.** *(Restaurant social media benchmarks, 2024)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- *(Baseline already held by client: ~60% of consumers use Instagram to find new restaurants — corroborated by Restroworks, 2025.)*

### Creator / UGC trust vs brand content

- **79% of people say UGC highly impacts their buying decisions — vs 13% for brand-created content and 8% for influencer content.** *(Stackla Consumer Content Report)* `[Global]` — **Med** — note the striking implication: **customer content beats paid influencer content by ~10x on stated impact.**
- **Only 9% of consumers see branded content as truly authentic; 56% say they'd be more influenced by a photo or video from a real customer than any other content format.** *(Stackla)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **92% of consumers trust UGC more than brand-created advertising.** *(Attributed to Nielsen; widely republished 2025–2026)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(the specific Nielsen study is not retrievable; the durable Nielsen finding is that ~83–92% trust recommendations from people they know)*
- **Shoppers 2.5x more likely to trust UGC than brand content; UGC performs ~8.7x better than professional content on engagement.** *(UGC vendor aggregation, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Low**

### Save / share value

- **Sends per reach is the strongest signal for reaching new audiences.** Mosseri has stated **sends carry roughly 3–5x more weight than likes** in deciding whether to push content to non-followers. *(Adam Mosseri / Instagram, 2025–2026)* `[Global]` — **High** *(platform-head statement, not a vendor claim)*
- **Watch time is the single most important signal across surfaces;** Instagram publicly confirmed its three heaviest ranking factors in January 2025. *(Adam Mosseri / Instagram, January 2025)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Saves are weighted meaningfully higher than likes**, signalling lasting value. *(Instagram, 2025–2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Food & Beverage Instagram engagement rate benchmark: 2.0–2.5%** to aim for; **average for food & cooking creators 2.3%**, top decile of nano accounts **7.2%+**. F&B ranks among the highest-engaging industries at **2.5–4.1%**. *(Dash Social, Snippet, 2024–2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

**Read-through for Fillo:** optimise for **sends and saves, not likes** — this is platform-confirmed, not inferred. And route budget toward customer UGC over paid influencers: the Stackla split (79% vs 8%) is the clearest signal in the category, and it happens to be the cheaper option.

## 7. Loyalty / subscription / referral

### Loyalty spend lift

- **Loyalty members spend 40% more per visit and visit 64% more often than non-members.** *(Study of 450+ restaurants, cited by Square, 2024)* `[Global]` — **Med** *(largest disclosed restaurant sample found)*
- **Loyalty members typically spend ~20% more per check and visit ~20% more frequently.** *(QSR industry aggregates, 2024)* `[Global]` — **Med** *(more conservative; the 20% and 40% figures bracket a plausible planning range)*
- **Taco Bell:** loyalty members spend **40% more annually**; members who actively redeem rewards spend **3.1x more** than those who don't. *(Taco Bell / Yum! Brands reporting, 2024)* `[Global]` — **Med** — the redemption gap matters more than enrolment: **enrolment alone does little; redemption behaviour is the real driver.**
- **Gamified elements (points multipliers, milestone challenges) see up to 30% higher engagement.** *(Voucherify, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Low**
- **85% of QSR loyalty members say the programme is about saving money, not VIP perks.** *(Alchemer QSR study, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Subscription economics — corroboration of the client's 2.5–3x LTV assumption

- **Subscription LTV is typically 3–5x higher than one-time DTC at the same gross margin.** *(Eightx DTC benchmarks, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Food subscriptions specifically deliver a 3–4x LTV lift over one-time purchases.** *(Eightx, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med** — ✅ **Fillo's 2.5–3x assumption is corroborated and is, if anything, slightly conservative. Recommend keeping 2.5–3x as the planning figure** — the mechanism below explains why the higher end is hard to reach.
- **Mechanism:** a one-time buyer averages **1–1.5 orders**; a subscription customer averages **8–18 orders before churn**. *(Eightx, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **45% of replenishment subscribers had been subscribed for at least one year.** *(McKinsey subscription commerce research)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Replenishment subscriptions churn structurally lower than discovery/curation subscriptions** because the underlying need persists regardless of interest. *(SubJolt / Eightx, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med** — relevant to Fillo's format choice: a weekly bread/staple subscription will retain better than a surprise-box format.
- **Churn thresholds:** below **5% monthly** = a durable subscription business; **5–8%** = manageable with a growth ceiling. **Most churn concentrates in the first two billing cycles** — if month-2 retention is below 70%, later-month improvements cannot rescue LTV. *(Eightx / SubJolt, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Food & beverage is the largest subscription-commerce category at over 30% market share.** *(Swell, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Referral benchmarks

- **Median ecommerce referral conversion rate 3–5%; top quartile 8%+.** *(ReferralCandy benchmarks, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Share rate (% of customers who share a referral link after buying): healthy range 5–15%.** *(ReferralCandy / Rivo, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Referred customers have 16–25% higher LTV and ~37% better retention** than non-referred. *(Extole / Rivo referral statistics, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Referral leads convert ~30% higher than leads from any other channel.** *(Referral marketing aggregates, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Low**
- **Referral CAC averages $45 in ecommerce vs $74 paid search and $87 influencer.** *(Rivo, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(US cost base; not transferable to India in absolute terms, but the ~40–50% CAC discount vs paid is directionally useful)*

## 8. On-site conversion elements

### Reviews / social proof widgets

- **Displaying reviews increases conversion by up to 270%.** Specifically, **purchase probability for a product with five reviews is 270% higher than for a product with none.** *(Spiegel Research Center, Northwestern University, 2017)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **Lift is steepest between 0 and 5 reviews** — going from zero to five produces a larger percentage lift than going from 5 to 50. *(Spiegel Research Center, 2017)* `[Global]` — **High** — actionable: **the first five reviews per SKU are worth disproportionate effort.**
- **Higher-priced items see a 380% conversion lift from reviews vs 190% for lower-priced items.** *(Spiegel Research Center, 2017)* `[Global]` — **High** — relevant to premium/gifting SKUs.
- **Purchase likelihood peaks at 4.0–4.7 stars, not 5.0** — perfect ratings read as inauthentic. *(Spiegel Research Center, 2017)* `[Global]` — **High**
- **UGC-enabled product pages generate 161% higher conversion rates than pages without UGC.** *(Bazaarvoice, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**

### Site speed

- **A 1-second delay in page load reduces conversions by ~7%.** *(Widely replicated; Akamai/Cloudflare lineage, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **A 1-second delay in mobile load times can impact conversion by up to 20%; a 3-second delay ~20% reduction.** *(Cloudflare / performance aggregates, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Conversion rates run ~40% at a 1-second load, dropping to ~29% by the third second.** *(Performance aggregation, 2026)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(the absolute conversion levels are implausibly high for ecommerce — the shape of the decay is the usable part, not the levels)*

### Delivery / pincode checker

- **No quantified data found on pincode/delivery-area checker impact on bounce rate.** **Gap.** The nearest evidence is indirect: **24.2% of European consumers say they wouldn't reorder from a store that doesn't offer reliable tracking** *(Sendcloud, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**, which speaks to delivery-information transparency generally rather than to a pre-purchase serviceability checker. For a Bengaluru-only D2C brand this element is best justified on expectation-setting logic rather than benchmark data — treat any number as an internal estimate and A/B test it.

## 9. Delivery-moment experience

- **85% of online shoppers say a poor delivery experience would prevent them from ordering from that retailer again.** *(Ipsos, e-commerce marketplaces and delivery experience study)* `[Global]` — **High** *(named research house)*
- **50% of shoppers stopped buying from a brand after a negative delivery experience;** among frequent/"power" shoppers this rises to **68%**, vs **42%** for regular shoppers. *(Bringg consumer research, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med** — the power-shopper skew matters: **your best customers are the least forgiving of delivery failure.**
- **84% of consumers will not return to an online store after just one poor delivery experience.** *(Chain Store Age survey coverage, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **When delivery goes well, 65% of shoppers say they'd buy from that retailer again even at a higher price than a competitor.** *(Bringg, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med** — the positive-side mirror of the above, and the more useful framing for a premium bakery.
- **57.4% of customers globally won't buy again if a package fails to arrive; 50.9% of European consumers would reconsider buying after receiving damaged goods.** *(Sendcloud, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med** — damage sensitivity is directly relevant to a bakery shipping fragile product.

### Handwritten notes and insert cards

- **A photocopy of a handwritten note increased subsequent customer spending by 100.5%** — control group spent **$25.97**, photocopied-handwritten-note group spent **$52.07**. An **original** handwritten note performed similarly to the photocopy (i.e. **photocopying does not destroy the effect** — this scales). A **machine-written note** (Malgun Gothic font) performed **no better than no note at all**. Field experiment on **1,232 customers** of a South Korean online beauty retailer, 21–28 February 2017. *(Sanghwa Kim, Jeonghye Choi, Seung Hyun Kim, "Do Handwritten Notes Benefit Online Retailers? A Field Experiment," Journal of Interactive Marketing, July 2022)* `[Global]` — **High** — ⭐ **peer-reviewed randomised field experiment; the highest-quality evidence for any single tactic in this document.**
- ⚠️ **Critical caveat from the same study: the positive effect disappears when the note is combined with a discount or promotion,** and an added giveaway weakens it. The mechanism is perceived warmth and authenticity — **stacking incentives destroys it.** — **High**
- **Everlane reported a 12% retention increase in 2024 after adding handwritten notes to shipments.** *(Vendor case citation, 2024)* `[Global]` — **Low** *(unverified brand case)*

## 10. In-person sampling / trial

- **Sampling conversion rates run 15–35%, with warehouse-club food demos at 25–35%; cross-category industry average ~20%.** *(In-store sampling industry analysis, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Sampled products see a 200–500% sales lift during the demo event** vs non-demo days. *(Sampling industry aggregation, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **Category-specific in-store demo lifts: beer +71%, wine +300%, frozen pizza up to +600%.** *(Sampling industry data, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- **In-store sampling increases purchase rates 35–75% during demo days; 73% of consumers say they're more likely to buy after trying a free sample.** *(Sampling aggregation, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med**
- ⚠️ **Important counter-finding: long-term conversion — the share of samplers who become repeat buyers — is poorly measured across the industry and is likely far lower than vendor claims.** In-store sampling is a **$2 billion channel with no agreed measurement standard.** *(IN·K /GROUND retail shopper analysis, 2025)* `[Global]` — **Med** — ⭐ **the most important caveat in this section. Same-day lift is real and well-replicated; durable conversion is unproven. Fillo should instrument this itself — capture contact at the van and measure 30/60/90-day repeat rather than trusting the 20% figure.**
- **India, van-based sampling:** 1 branded van × 30 days → **40,000+ samples distributed at a claimed 15% trial-to-purchase conversion.** *(The Mediaverse Bangalore case study, 2026)* `[India]` — **Low** *(vendor-reported, unaudited — but the only India-specific van-sampling datapoint found, and structurally the closest analogue to Fillo's model)*
- **Queue / scarcity psychology: no quantified conversion data found.** **Gap.** Related evidence exists in academic behavioural literature but no benchmark suitable for a prioritisation matrix surfaced in this search.

### Named gaps

Five things were searched for and not found. Each needs either an internal estimate or first-party measurement:

1. **Festive share of annual bakery sales in India** — category demand is well-evidenced, bakery-specific seasonality is not published.
2. **India-specific review-reading rate for food businesses** — only a quality-sensitivity proxy (LocalCircles, 2019) is available.
3. **Pincode/delivery-checker impact on bounce** — no quantified study exists; A/B test it.
4. **Queue/scarcity conversion benchmarks** — behavioural literature exists, usable benchmarks do not.
5. **Packaging-design-only willingness-to-pay lift** — all available WTP data measures sustainability or provenance premiums, not design.
6. **Food-truck-specific branding effectiveness** — no study found; the Bengaluru van-sampling case is the only close analogue and it is vendor-reported.
