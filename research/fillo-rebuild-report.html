01

Where the site is losing money today

Six problems. Two of them are broken promises rather than missing features, and no amount of new design fixes them.

The price changes at the door

The site promises ₹420 and Shopify then asks for ₹470, because delivery is added after the customer has already committed. An unexpected cost at the end is the single best documented cause of cart abandonment. Hotplate shows subtotal, taxes and total at checkout step one, before an address exists; Zepto puts zero-fee promises in its hero. Fillo has to compute delivery on its own checkout, from the first render.

The site never asks where you are

A customer can build a cart, pick a date, choose a slot, fill in their contact details and reach Shopify before discovering the van does not come to their street. For a business whose entire proposition is a fixed route, where are you is the first question the site should ask and it is currently the only one it never asks. Worse, an out-of-area lookup is not a lost customer for a van, it is route-planning data, and the site throws that signal away.

Two checkouts, two domains, one funnel

The purchase starts on fillobakes.com and finishes on myshopify.com. Levain and Theobroma both split their funnels the same way and both are worse for it, and neither of them changes domain mid-purchase the way Fillo does.

Numbers that are not true

100+ Items against a menu of 23. 500+ Lovers on the home page against thousands of loyal customers on the franchise page, two invented figures contradicting each other. A dummy phone number, +91 98765 43210, has been publicly live on the franchise page. Separately, Google currently appears to show a buyer the website price of ₹200 and a Swiggy price of ₹249 for the same loaf on the same screen. That needs verifying, and if it holds it is the most damaging integrity problem on the list.

Dead content, indexed

Eight of the twelve product URLs in the sitemap render Product Not Found at HTTP 200, so Google has indexed soft 404s. All 23 blog posts are duplicates of the shop descriptions and every Read More link points at an undefined route. No core page has a title tag, meta description, canonical or structured data. The shipping policy describes couriers, weight-based charges and one to two business days of processing, which is a warehouse, not a van.

The one thing nobody else has is hidden in a corner

Fillo runs a live GPS feed of a moving bakery, updating every fifteen seconds. Nobody else in this study has anything like it, and it is the only capability here a competitor cannot buy off a Shopify app store. Today it is a floating pill that opens a modal.

What we are building

One checkout on one domain where the total never moves. Area and lane decided before the cart and carried in the header from then on. A computed cutoff sentence in three places. The van promoted from a pill to a bookmarkable page whose off-air state is as finished as its live state. Fillo+ made free and phone-based, with a real weekly bread subscription sold at order two.

04

The recommended journey

Three ways in, one spine. This is a recommendation, not a menu of options.

Lane A · first order, step by step

  1. Arrive. The announcement bar carries a real, computed deadline. The header chip is empty and amber: Set your area.
  2. Choose the lane. Two cards above the menu with true prices. Catch the van, free, at a stop near you, or home delivery, ₹49, in a two-hour window, free over ₹499. Choosing sets the chip to place, mode and next slot. Out of area opens a waitlist with a position number. The catalogue is never blocked. Only the answer is gated.
  3. Browse. This week's bake, six to eight items, sold-out greyed in place with a Notify me. All 23 are one tap behind it. Search is present but is not the hero. The three doors module sits at the top of the shop page, never as a blocking screen.
  4. Decide. On the product page: crumb photo first, one sensory line, checkable spec chips, eggless stated as the reason rather than the headline, a fixed allergen block, reviews for that item, and the cutoff line in red directly above the button.
  5. Cart. The drawer shows subtotal, the delivery line or free, you are catching the van, the gap to free delivery, and the total. During a live run a hold timer runs, because the stock really is held.
  6. Checkout. One page, one domain, four blocks in this order: lane and slot, pre-filled from the chip with two or three named run cards instead of a calendar. Then phone and OTP, with email optional and labelled for your invoice. Then address, home-delivery lane only, India-shaped with flat, building, area, landmark and pincode. Then pay, with the same total as the drawer, UPI first and cash last and priced.
  7. Confirm. A promise with a named next contact, not a warning. Saturday, 4 to 6pm, at your gate. We will message you Friday night with the exact time. Plus a tracker link and a one-tap free Fillo+ join, since the phone is already verified.

Lane B · the repeat order becomes a standing order

Do not sell the subscription on a first visit, and never in the hero. Sell it on the confirmation of the second order, when the habit already exists and the pitch is a statement of fact: you have ordered a Milk Shokupan twice. Want it on the van every Saturday? Skip any week. The builder is four steps on one screen, and the route day is derived from the saved area rather than asked for. After that the loop is four messages: Wednesday preview with a skip button, Thursday 8pm cutoff and charge, Friday night time band, Saturday bake strip through to delivered.

Lane C · the street encounter

This is the journey the current site does not serve at all, and it is where a moving bakery's demand actually starts. Someone sees the van or is handed a card, scans the QR on the glass case, and lands on the van page with no login, no app and no location permission. If the van is out they see stops and a band. If it is not, they see this week's route and a Notify me. One phone field starts a WhatsApp opt-in, and Saturday morning brings one message with a deep link into the drop. Because the phone is the account and they picked the van lane, no address is ever asked for.

05

The van tracker

The list owns the truth. The map owns the feeling. Every number on the page must be readable with the map layer completely failed.

Off-air is the default state, so it gets designed first

If the van runs two or three days a week, a live map is right on a minority of days. The Hotplate storefront is off-air roughly ninety percent of the time and remains a page worth bookmarking, because the schedule and the next drop are the content. That is the structural model. The schedule is the page. The live element is the garnish.

Stops, not minutes. Bands, not times.

Distance in metres is a lie on a multi-stop Bengaluru route, and a single arrival minute is a promise the traffic will break. Country Delight and Milkbasket both publish windows and never a minute. Proximity should be counted in stops. We should be honest that the specific stops-away threshold is a launch guess to be tuned, not a measured figure, because the Amazon implementation it comes from is order-scoped and neither we nor the prior research has watched it run. What survives every failure mode is the stop list.

The privacy contract, as a hard requirement

A public, always-on, precise position of a small vehicle carrying cash and one or two staff is a safety surface, and the driver's whole working day should not be public. So: the public endpoint returns a position snapped to the route plus a stop index, rounded to about fifty metres, and it is served only between the route's start and end times. No history trail is ever exposed. If the van leaves the published route the position is suppressed and the page shows between stops. The driver has a Go dark switch in the cab. If a fix is more than ninety seconds stale, the marker fades and the band is replaced with a last-seen line, because a stale dot pretending to be live destroys the credibility the page exists to earn. Notify-me geofences run server-side against the van, so the customer never grants a location permission. The line under the sheet says it plainly: we watch the van, not you.

What we could not observe here, and why it matters

The Domino's four-stage strip and its display-latency cap, Amazon's stops-away counter and its fallback wording, Swiggy and Zomato's marker interpolation, and Mister Softee's truck tracker are all either order-scoped, app-only or unreachable. They are inherited from prior research and cited as such. What we could observe is the surrounding layer, and it turned out to be the more transferable half: Hotplate publishing a route as plain text in a bio, Theobroma running a locator where each stop carries its own status and hours, Swiggy rendering out-of-area inline instead of as an error page, Blinkit and Zepto reserving the header slot for delivery status, and Country Delight and Milkbasket writing arrival as a window. Note also that the widely quoted WhatsApp open-rate figures come from vendor marketing blogs, so we plan against the more conservative broadcast conversion range instead.

Fillo+ and The Standing Order

The current site puts a ₹1 charge, a coins scheme and a would-be subscription under one label. They are three different things and they need splitting.

LayerWhat it isPriceSold when
Fillo+The free membership and account layer. Phone-based identity carrying your orders, addresses, saved stop, coins, alerts and early access to new bakes.FreeOne tap from any order confirmation, where the phone is already verified.
Fillo Coins2 coins per ₹100 spent, 25 coins redeem as ₹25 off, no expiry. Unchanged mechanics, moved behind the free membership.FreeAutomatic once a member.
The Standing OrderThe weekly bread subscription. A recurring pre-order against a route day, not a shipped box. Weekly by default, fortnightly optional, charged per delivery.Per delivery, at a standing priceOrder #2 confirmation. Never order #1.

Why the ₹1 join fee comes off

Nobody in this study charges to join a loyalty programme. Country Delight gives free membership on signup, GAIL's runs a free nine-stamp card, Levain has the Cookie Club, Wildgrain gives croissants for life. Fillo's ₹1 currently requires the customer to complete an entire Shopify checkout in order to join a free-to-operate retention mechanic, which filters out most of the people it exists to capture.

Existing ₹1 payers are grandfathered, not devalued. They keep their coins, they get a permanent named Founding member badge, and they get first access to every new bake. Legacy email-only members get one message asking them to add a phone number so their coins carry over, and the old dashboard route redirects into the new rewards page. The change has to read as a gift, not as a refund.

Why the subscription is a separate product

It has its own price, its own cadence, its own management surface and its own failure modes: out of stock, payment failure, route change. Calling it Fillo+ would make cancel Fillo+ ambiguous between stop my bread and leave the programme, which is a genuine support problem. The Standing Order is a real bakery phrase, it says exactly what it is, and it survives being said out loud at a van window. Do not call it a box: it is not shipped, and the word imports the wrong expectations.

Route-day awareness is the whole differentiator

The product is not a box every week. It is you are on Saturday's Indiranagar list. So the builder only offers days the van actually serves the saved area, the reminder names the stop, the confirmation names the stop, and if a route changes every affected subscriber is messaged before the cutoff. Every control is self-serve: skip one week, pause with a return date, change the day or route, change the contents, change frequency, change stop or address including a one-off override, and cancel in one tap with pause offered once and no retention maze. If a loaf cannot be baked, the customer is notified at cutoff, offered a named substitute, and not charged unless they accept. On a forty-loaf bake this will happen constantly, so it needs designing rather than handling by message.

The line that goes above every subscribe button

Skip any week. Pause anytime. Cancel in one tap. We will message you every Wednesday with what is coming, so you are never surprised.

Contradictions inside the prior corpus, now settled

The cutoff was variously 6pm, 8pm and 9pm across three documents. It is now Thursday 8pm, computed once and rendered identically everywhere. The delivery fee was ₹50 in one place and ₹49 with free delivery over ₹399 in another; the fee is ₹49 and the threshold is pending your confirmation. Prices show the rupee symbol rather than bare numerals. The invented driver name has been removed entirely: if there is a real person on the route we would like to name them, but we will not invent one.
